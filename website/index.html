Put a DVD in.
Get it in Plex.

Changeover turns a shelf of discs into a Plex library. Put one in and it works out what the film is, rips it, files it the way Plex expects, and ejects the disc so you can load the next one.

Smart import

It works out what the disc is, and shows you how

A DVD was designed to be read by a person sitting in front of a television. Put one in and you see a menu: the film's title, Play Movie, a grid of scenes, a list of languages. You know instantly what the disc is and what to press.

A computer sees none of that. A DVD menu is not a screen of text — it is a little loop of video with the words painted into the picture. There is nothing to read. What a computer gets instead is a volume label like BASKBALL and a list of two dozen numbered titles, one of which is the film and the rest of which are trailers, logos, menu loops and sometimes the same film again in a cropped transfer.

That gap is why ripping a shelf of discs has always been manual work: read the label, guess, search, compare runtimes, pick a title number, start the encode — for every disc. Smart Import closes it. Changeover looks at the menu the way you would, works out which film it is, and starts the import on its own — in about fifteen seconds, showing its work as it goes: 

▶ Reading the disc's menus — 21 pages to look at
  · vtsm-01-lu1-pgc1: PLAY MOVIE · SCENE SELECTION · SPECIAL FEATURES
  · vtsm-01-lu1-pgc28: SCENES · 1-4 5-8 9-12 · MAIN MENU
▶ Identifying the disc — its label reads "BASKBALL"
  · "Baskball" found nothing on TMDB — looking at the disc's menus
  · asking this Mac's on-device model to read 34 lines of menu text
  · the model reads it as "BASEketball"
▶ Matching against TMDB — the disc's feature runs 103 min
  · BASEketball — 103 min · Trey Parker, Matt Stone
  · Baseketball Legends — 48 min
✓ Chose BASEketball — its title matches and its runtime fits the disc

Four steps, and each one is doing a job a person would otherwise do by hand.

  1. It renders the menus and reads them. Each menu screen is turned into a still image, and Vision — the same text recognition that reads a receipt in Photos — reads the words off it. This is the step that gives a computer eyes: chapter names, languages, and often the film's own title, recovered from pixels.
  2. It works out what that means. Apple's on-device model turns BASKBALL — a label missing two letters — into BASEketball, and reads menu text to name a film whose label says nothing useful at all.
  3. It confirms against TMDB. A guess is not enough, so the title is checked against two facts the disc supplies by itself: how long the feature actually runs, and whether the billed cast appears anywhere on the menus. Two films of the same name are separated by runtime; a label that is an actor's name is settled by the cast.
  4. Then it runs the import. The right title is selected — not the trailer the disc claims is the main feature, not the cropped transfer — encoded, filed as Movies/Title (Year) {tmdb-ID}/Title (Year).mp4, and the disc is ejected so you can load the next one.

When the evidence is thin it says so and lets you choose, rather than guessing on your behalf. That is the difference between automation you can leave running and automation you have to check.

And the rest of it

Picks the right part

A DVD holds trailers, menus, and sometimes the same film twice — once widescreen, once cropped for an old television. Changeover finds the feature, prefers the widescreen transfer, and refuses to encode a two-minute trailer just because the disc called it the main title.

Files it the way Plex wants

Movies/Title (Year) {tmdb-ID}/Title (Year).mp4, every time, with the database id that lets Plex tell two films of the same name apart. Different cuts of one film sit side by side as editions.

Runs without you

It ejects each disc when it's done, so the open tray is the signal to load the next one. It keeps working on a Mac whose screen has locked — including discs put in after the lock, which macOS would otherwise spit straight back out.

What you need

  • A Mac running macOS 26.2 or later, with a USB DVD drive.
  • HandBrake's command-line tool: brew install handbrake
  • A free TMDB API key, for looking films up.
  • Somewhere to put the files — a Plex library folder, local or on a NAS.

Changeover only reads discs you own, and only the ones you put in the drive.
