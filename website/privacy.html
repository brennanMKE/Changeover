Privacy

Changeover has no accounts, no analytics and no server of its own. It runs on your Mac, reads discs you put in the drive, and writes files where you tell it.

What leaves your Mac

  • Film searches, to TMDB. To work out which film is on a disc, Changeover sends a search term — usually the disc's label, sometimes a title read from its menus — to themoviedb.org, using your own API key. It then fetches runtime, cast and poster for the results shown. TMDB's own privacy policy covers what they do with a request.
  • Update checks, to this website. Release builds ask changeover.sstools.co/appcast.xml whether a newer version exists. That is an ordinary web request: it carries no identifier and nothing about your library. Sparkle asks your permission before the first automatic check, and you can turn it off.

That is the complete list. Nothing else is sent anywhere.

What stays on your Mac

  • Your TMDB key, and the folders you chose, in the app's own settings.
  • The films themselves, wherever you pointed Changeover — a local disk or your own server.
  • A record of each disc it read: the label, the film you picked, the disc's structure, and text read off its menus. This stays in a folder you choose and is never uploaded. It exists so the app can be tested against real discs, and you can delete it at any time.

What it never does

  • No telemetry, crash reporting or usage statistics.
  • No account, no sign-in, no licence check.
  • It reads only the disc in the drive, and only when you put one in.

On-device intelligence

When a disc's label needs interpreting — THESECRETLIFEOFWALTERMITTY into The Secret Life of Walter Mitty — Changeover uses Apple's on-device model. That runs entirely on your Mac. Nothing about the disc is sent to Apple or to anyone else.
