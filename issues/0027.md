# 0027 — Audio and subtitle languages cannot be chosen anywhere in the app

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | open |
| **Module** | MetadataEntryView / AppSettings |
| **Platform** | macOS |
| **First seen** | 2026-09-11 |

## Description

`mac_plex_dvd_workflow.md` §3.3 says to keep English and Spanish audio and drop the
rest. Changeover offers no way to express that — there is no track UI, no language
preference, and the HandBrake invocation is a fixed string
(`Changeover/EncodeController.swift:20-28`). The user gets whatever the defaults
produce.

## Long Description

This ticket is the *choosing*; [#0029](0029.md) is the *enforcing*. They are split
because the UI is testable and the encoder behaviour needs a disc, and because the
request shape defined here is protocol surface that Phase 4 inherits directly —
`RemoteControl.md` lines 324–329 already sketches it:

```swift
struct RipRequest: Codable {
    let metadata: MovieMetadata
    let titleIndices: [Int]
    let audioLanguages: [String]      // e.g. ["eng", "spa"]
    let subtitleLanguages: [String]
}
```

That sketch is superseded on one point: `titleIndices: [Int]` becomes a single
feature index plus a typed list of extras. See the plan and [#0031](0031.md).

Defining that type now, in the host app, and shaping the UI to produce it means the
Phase 4 package move is a file drag rather than a redesign of how a job is
described.

One finding from planning that should not be buried: **most DVD subtitles cannot be
carried into an MP4 as selectable tracks.** DVD subtitles are usually VOBSUB —
bitmap images — and MP4 supports only text-based subtitle tracks. HandBrake will
either drop them or require burning them into the picture. So "pick subtitle
languages" cannot mean the same thing as "pick audio languages" for this app's
output format, and the UI must not imply otherwise. `Roadmap.md` line 119 pairs them
as if they were symmetric; they are not. See [#0029](0029.md), which the fixtures
narrowed: `S_CC608/DVD` streams are converted to **text** by MakeMKV and are not
blocked by MP4, so the deferral applies to bitmap subs only.

### What the experiment changed here

`DiscSelection.md` moved work *out* of this ticket and *into* it in roughly equal
measure.

**Out: multi-title selection as a way of finding the movie.** `RipRequest` was
sketched with `titleIndices: [Int]` on the theory that the user might select several
titles to work out which one is the feature. [#0025](0025.md) now identifies the
feature from a single duration comparison plus a Play All guard (9/9 discs), so
the feature is **one
index**. Multiple titles survive only as *extras*, which have a different
destination ([#0031](0031.md)) and therefore belong in a different field with a
different type — not in a flat array where an extra and the feature are
indistinguishable.

**In: track selection turned out to be the genuinely hard half.** Title selection
was one comparison; every multi-track disc in the corpus carries duplicates that
language alone cannot separate. Three findings, each with fixture evidence:

1. **Language tags are optional.** `hornets-nest-min0.txt` contains **zero**
   attribute-3 lines across the whole file. Its feature (title 9) has two
   `DD Surround 5.1` audio tracks and one VOBSUB subtitle, none of them tagged with
   a language, and attribute 30 renders as `"DD Surround 5.1"` with no language
   suffix and `""` for the subtitle. A preference filter of `["eng","spa"]` matches
   nothing here and would drop every audio track, producing a **silent MP4**. The
   rule: *if no stream on the title carries a language tag, keep everything.*
2. **Duplicates are the norm, and deduplication is required.**
   `dragon-tattoo-min0.txt` title 0 streams 1 and 3 are both
   `DD Surround 5.1 English` at 448 Kb/s, 6 channels, `A_AC3`, differing only in the
   default flag (attribute 38: `"d"` versus `""`). `hanna-min0.txt` title 3 streams
   4 and 5 are both `DD Stereo English` at 192 Kb/s. Deduplicate on
   `(languageCode, codecId, bitrate, displayName)` and keep the first occurrence —
   which is also the one carrying the default flag on Dragon Tattoo.
3. **Attribute 22 partially solves commentary detection — and partially does not.**
   On `supertroopers-min0.txt` title 0, streams 4 and 5 are both `DD Stereo English`
   192 Kb/s but carry `22 = "1"` and `22 = "2"` where the feature audio carries
   `22 = "0"`: a cast commentary and a director commentary, flagged by MakeMKV.
   `hanna-min0.txt` title 3 stream 5 carries `22 = "1"` where the otherwise
   byte-identical stream 4 carries `0`. **But it misses cases**: on
   `super-troopers-2-min0.txt` title 0, streams 1 and 2 are both
   `DD Surround 5.1 English` at 448 and 384 Kb/s with `22 = "0"` on both — the
   384 Kb/s track is almost certainly a commentary and nothing in the metadata says
   so. So: treat a non-zero, non-4096 attribute-22 value as a commentary signal
   worth *excluding by default and showing in the list*, and accept that a
   bitrate-differing duplicate in the same language remains **unsolved**. Do not
   invent a "lower bitrate means commentary" rule — Dragon Tattoo's two 448 Kb/s
   English 5.1 tracks would break it in the other direction.

**Forced subtitles are detectable, and they are the one subtitle class worth
keeping** for a Plex movie, because they caption foreign dialogue in an otherwise
English film. Detect them from **attribute 22 == 4096**, not by string-matching
`"(forced only)"` out of attribute 30. All ten occurrences in the corpus are
`S_VOBSUB` streams — e.g. `SINFO:0,6,22,0,"4096"` alongside
`SINFO:0,6,30,0," English  (forced only)"` in `super-troopers-2-min0.txt`. Attribute
30 is a localized display string; attribute 22 is a number.

## Steps to reproduce

1. Insert a DVD that carries English, Spanish and French audio.
2. Search the title, pick the poster, and look for a track list.

## Expected behavior

Once the feature title is settled, its actual audio and subtitle streams are listed
with their languages, deduplicated, with commentary tracks visible but unselected
and the user's preferred languages preselected. A title whose streams carry no
language tags at all keeps everything rather than nothing. The selection is carried
into the job as an explicit `RipRequest` naming one feature index and, optionally,
extras bound for a destination outside the Plex roots.

## Actual behavior

There is no track UI. `MetadataEntryView`
(`Changeover/MetadataEntryView.swift:11-26`) has a search bar, results list, folder
preview, log and a start button. `startRipping()` (`:142-155`) constructs a
`DVDPipeline` from a `MovieMetadata` and `AppSettings` and nothing else, and
HandBrake is invoked with a hard-coded `--aencoder copy:aac,copy:ac3 --subtitle scan`
(`Changeover/EncodeController.swift:24-26`).

## Notes

Two genuinely different verification stories, and the ticket should be honest about
which is which.

Unit-testable with no disc, and the fixtures make all of it concrete:

- **The no-language fallback** — parse `hornets-nest-min0.txt`, take title 9, apply
  a preference of `["eng","spa"]`, and assert **both** audio tracks survive. This is
  the silent-movie test and it should be written first.
- **Deduplication** — `dragon-tattoo-min0.txt` title 0 has five audio streams that
  reduce to three distinct offerings; `hanna-min0.txt` title 3 has six that reduce
  to four (or five, depending on whether the commentary is folded in — decide and
  pin it).
- **Forced-subtitle detection** — `super-troopers-2-min0.txt` title 0 streams 6, 8
  and 10 are forced (attribute 22 == 4096) and streams 5, 7, 9 are not. Assert the
  classifier reads attribute 22 and **not** attribute 30, e.g. by feeding a
  synthetic stream with `22 = 0` and `30 = " English  (forced only)"` and asserting
  it is *not* forced.
- **Commentary flagging** — `supertroopers-min0.txt` title 0 streams 4 and 5.
- The intersection of preferred languages with available ones; `RipRequest` JSON
  round-trip; `AppSettings` load/persist of the preferred-language lists.

Needs a disc: that the languages listed match what MakeMKV.app shows for the same
title. Whether the chosen languages actually survive into the `.mp4` belongs to
[#0029](0029.md) and should be verified there, not claimed here.

### `RipRequest`

Match `RemoteControl.md` lines 324–329, with two changes:

```swift
nonisolated struct RipRequest: Codable, Hashable, Sendable {
    var metadata: MovieMetadata
    /// The one title identified as the movie. Not an array — see #0025.
    var featureTitleIndex: Int
    /// Titles the user opted into as extras. Never routed to the Plex roots — see #0031.
    var extraTitleIndices: [Int]
    /// The --minlength the scan ran with; the rip must use the same value. See #0028.
    var scanMinLengthSeconds: Int
    var audioLanguages: [String]
    var subtitleLanguages: [String]
}
```

Three deliberate departures from the `RemoteControl.md` sketch:

- **`featureTitleIndex: Int`, not `titleIndices: [Int]`.** [#0025](0025.md)
  identifies one title. An array invites a caller to iterate it into `Movies/`.
- **Extras are a separate field**, because their destination is different. The
  destination itself is typed in [#0031](0031.md) rather than being a path string
  either field carries.
- **`scanMinLengthSeconds` travels with the request.** A title index is meaningless
  without the threshold it was produced at — `hanna-min0.txt` and
  `hanna-mindefault.txt` both contain a title 1 named `D1_t01.mkv`, one 13 seconds
  long and one the whole 1:50:45 movie. Carrying the threshold in the request is
  what makes [#0028](0028.md)'s "never mix indices across scans" rule enforceable
  rather than a convention, and Phase 4 needs it in the wire format for the same
  reason.

`nonisolated` for the same reason as [#0022](0022.md): the project sets
`SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION = MainActor`
(`Changeover.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj:416,445`), and this value has to reach
`RipController` and `EncodeController`, both `nonisolated static`
(`Changeover/RipController.swift:9`, `Changeover/EncodeController.swift:9`). And
`Codable` requires `MovieMetadata` to become `Codable` too —
`Changeover/MovieMetadata.swift:5-26` is currently a plain struct with a
`TMDBMovie`-only initializer (`:10-14`). Add `Codable, Hashable, Sendable` and a
memberwise init alongside the existing one; it is three stored `String`s
(`:6-8`), so the synthesized conformance is free and Phase 4 needs it anyway.

Language codes are ISO 639-2 three-letter strings exactly as MakeMKV reports them in
attribute 3, so no mapping layer is needed between the scan and the request. Keep
them lowercase and normalize once on the way in. Note the corpus uses the
bibliographic codes — `fre` not `fra`, `chi` not `zho` — and
`the-girl-in-the-spider-s-web-min0.txt` title 0 alone carries `eng`, `khm`, `chi`,
`fre`, `kor`, `may`, `por`, `spa`, `tha` and `vie`. Do not assume a short set.

### Available languages

A pure function on the selected title:

```swift
extension DiscTitle {
    var audioLanguages: [LanguageOption]      // code + display name + track count
    var subtitleLanguages: [LanguageOption]
}
```

Derive from `streams` filtered by `DiscStream.kind`, grouped by `languageCode`, in
first-appearance order — which on a DVD is usually the disc's own priority order and
is a better default ordering than alphabetical.

**Deduplicate before grouping.** Collapse streams with an identical
`(languageCode, codecId, bitrate, displayName)` tuple to their first occurrence, and
keep the count so the UI can say "2 tracks". Without this, Dragon Tattoo offers the
same 448 Kb/s English 5.1 track twice and the user has no way to tell them apart.

**Flag commentary rather than hiding it.** A stream whose attribute-22 flags carry a
non-zero value other than 4096 is a commentary; show it in the list, labelled, and
leave it unselected by default. Accept that some commentaries are unflagged (Super
Troopers 2) and will be offered as ordinary tracks.

**The whole-title no-language fallback is not the same as a per-stream `nil`.** Two
distinct rules, and getting them confused is how the silent MP4 happens:

- A *single* stream with no language code among tagged siblings → offer it as an
  explicit "Undetermined" option, as before.
- *No stream on the title carries a language code at all* (Hornets' Nest) → the
  language preference does not apply to this title. Keep every audio track and say
  so in the UI: "this disc does not tag its audio languages — keeping all tracks".
  Do not silently fall back to "first track only", which looks identical in the UI
  and is wrong on a disc with an English and a Spanish track.

Use `Locale.current.localizedString(forLanguageCode:)` for the display name rather
than `DiscStream.languageName` (attribute 4), which is localized to MakeMKV's UI
language, not the user's — and which is absent entirely on an untagged disc.

### Preferences

Add to `Changeover/AppSettings.swift` next to the existing stored properties
(`:13-16`), with `Keys` (`:58-63`), `init()` loads (`:38-43`) and `persist()` writes
(`:48-54`):

```swift
var preferredAudioLanguages: [String] = ["eng", "spa"]
var preferredSubtitleLanguages: [String] = []
```

The `["eng", "spa"]` default is straight out of `mac_plex_dvd_workflow.md` §3.3 and
is the actual working default for this library. Note that the existing `init()`
pattern guards on `!v.isEmpty` for strings (`:39-42`); an array needs
`stringArray(forKey:)` and a `nil` check, and an *intentionally* empty list must
survive a round trip — which is why `preferredSubtitleLanguages` defaulting to empty
needs the `nil`-versus-empty distinction to be handled properly rather than with the
existing `isEmpty` idiom.

`SettingsView` (`Changeover/SettingsView.swift`) gets a field for each. A
comma-separated text field is adequate and matches the existing form's density; a
token field is nicer and can wait.

### The picker

`TrackSelectionView` sits under the selected title row from [#0026](0026.md), and
appears only when a title is selected. Two labelled groups — Audio, Subtitles — each
a list of toggles, one per available language, showing the track count per language
so a disc with two English audio tracks (stereo plus 5.1) is visible.

Preselection: intersect the title's available languages with the preferred lists. If
the intersection is empty for audio, select the first available track and say why —
silently producing a movie with no audio because the disc has no English is the worst
outcome available here, and it is the likeliest way this feature misfires on a
foreign-language disc.

For subtitles, surface the VOBSUB/MP4 constraint at the point of choosing rather than
letting the user discover it in the output — but the constraint is now
per-stream, not per-disc, and the picker should reflect that:

- **`S_CC608/DVD`** — MakeMKV converts these to text
  (`SINFO:3,1,30,0,"CC→Text English ( Lossy conversion )"` in
  `dragon-tattoo-min0.txt`), and MP4 carries text subtitles. Four of the seven
  discs have one. Selectable, subject to [#0029](0029.md) actually wiring it.
- **`S_VOBSUB` with attribute 22 == 4096** — forced. Worth keeping, but only by
  burning in, and only one can be burned. Offer it as a distinct choice, not as a
  language.
- **`S_VOBSUB` otherwise** — cannot be a selectable MP4 track. Show it, greyed, with
  the reason.

Do not ship a picker that quietly does nothing, and do not label the whole group as
unsupported when a third of the corpus has a text track in it.

State ownership follows [#0026](0026.md) and `Plan.md`'s conventions: the selection
lives on `JobController` ([#0001](0001.md)), the view takes it as a parameter and
uses `@Bindable` for the toggles — no `@State` copies, no `ObservableObject`.

### Threading the request through

`startRipping()` (`Changeover/MetadataEntryView.swift:142-155`) builds
`DVDPipeline(metadata:settings:log:)` today. Change `DVDPipeline`
(`Changeover/DVDPipeline.swift:9-13`) to hold a `RipRequest` instead of a bare
`metadata`, and keep `settings` as it is — the pipeline already captures the paths it
needs off `AppSettings` on the main actor before entering nonisolated code
(`:19-24`), which is the right pattern and should be preserved when the language
lists join them.

`metadata.folderName` / `.fileName` (`Changeover/MovieMetadata.swift:17-24`) are used
at `DVDPipeline.swift:17,39` and must keep working unchanged — reach them through
`request.metadata`.
