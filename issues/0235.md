|---|---|
| **Status** | resolved |
| **Phase** | 3 — Unattended multi-disc |

| **First seen** | 2026-10-07 |

## Description

REPO_MEN holds the Unrated cut (title 1, 1:59:28, 21 chapters) and the
Theatrical cut (title 2, 1:51:06, 21 chapters) of one film. It was classified
`multipleFeatures`, Start refused with `noFeatureChosen`, and the CLI was asked
to name a second film that does not exist. `select --movie 31867 --feature-title
1` was refused `refusedMovieNotFound` and only worked once `--query` was added.

## Why the existing rules cannot catch it

They are measurably too close to a real double feature:

| Disc | runtimes | ratio | chapters | truth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Repo Men | 1:59:28 / 1:51:06 | 0.930 | 21 / 21 | one film, two cuts |
| Uncle Buck / The Great Outdoors | 1:40 / 1:31 | 0.910 | 17 / 17 | two films |

Twenty thousandths apart on runtime and identical on chapters. Any threshold
that catches Repo Men catches Uncle Buck, and Uncle Buck's two films are exactly
what #0157's removed chapter test used to destroy. Nothing in the title list
separates them.

## Fix

A different question, of a different source: **does the menu offer these as
versions of one thing?** A disc holding two films names two films; a disc
holding two cuts names one film twice and distinguishes them with a word like
UNRATED or THEATRICAL. REPO_MEN's menu reads "PLAY UNRATED VERSION" / "PLAY
THEATRICAL VERSION". That word is the evidence and nothing else on the disc
carries it.

`AlternateCuts` holds the vocabulary and the matching. `severalFeatures` gained
a defaulted `menuLines:` parameter, so every caller without a menu behaves
exactly as before, and the check runs last — after every structural rule has
already decided this looks like several features — because it is the only rule
that can say "despite all that, one film".

Both halves are required: the menu must name **two different** version words
(one alone is a label, not a choice) and the groups must be close enough to be
cuts at all.

## Gotchas

Matching is on **whole words**, not substrings, and a test caught why:
"UNRATED" contains "rated", so with substring matching a menu offering only the
unrated cut scored two version words and would have collapsed a disc it should
not have touched. `rated` is now absent from the table entirely — a disc
offering both always names the unrated one.

## Relation

Leaves a gap worth its own ticket: alternate cuts map naturally onto Plex
`{edition-Unrated}` / `{edition-Theatrical}` naming, which neither the app nor
the CLI can express today. Detecting the cuts would also let the app *explain* a
runtime mismatch instead of only gating on it (#0234).
