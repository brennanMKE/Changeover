# 0246 — A multi-film disc that names its films is still not identified

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | open |
| **Phase** | 3 — Unattended multi-disc |
| **Module** | RipFlowController / FeatureMatching / MultiTitleInference |
| **Platform** | macOS |
| **First seen** | 2026-10-10 |

## Description

On a WILLIS triple feature the app reported `film — not identified` throughout,
on a disc that says what it holds. The volume label is useless (`WILLIS`), but
the main menu is nothing but the three titles:

```
Jackal | MERCURY | RISING | ALPHA DOG | SUBTITLES: ON/OFF
```

A TMDB search of each, constrained by runtime against the three features,
settles all three with no ambiguity:

| menu text | film | TMDB runtime | title | scanned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jackal | The Jackal (1997) | 124 m | 6 | 2:04:14 |
| ALPHA DOG | Alpha Dog (2006) | 122 m | 7 | 1:57:36 |
| MERCURY RISING | Mercury Rising (1998) | 111 m | 8 | 1:51:15 |

The agent did this by hand. **The app already has every input**: the menu OCR,
the feature list and a TMDB client.

## Why this is the highest-value gap open

The user's stated priority for this testing is that as many discs as possible
import automatically. This is the disc shape that stops that: a multi-film disc
needs a human (or an agent acting as one) to name each film, and that is the
only step on the whole path that cannot be automated today. With it, a disc
where some films are already in Plex could run end to end — identify, skip what
is held (#0245), import the rest, eject — with no chooser at all.

## What makes it harder than it looks

- **OCR splits a title across lines.** "MERCURY" and "RISING" arrive separately,
  so lines have to be joined before searching. Joining wrongly is how "ALPHA
  DOG SUBTITLES" becomes a search term.
- **The menu drops articles.** "Jackal", not "The Jackal". TMDB finds it anyway,
  but an exact-match rule would not.
- **Runtime is the arbiter, and it is loose.** Alpha Dog's TMDB runtime is 122 m
  against a 117:36 title — about four minutes out, and right only because it is
  the last one left. A tight runtime window would reject the correct answer.
- **The buttons cannot help.** They resolve only to `VMGM.pgc12` and `pgc14`, so
  pairing a label with a title needs the #0244 dispatch resolver. Runtime
  matching works without it **on this disc** because the three runtimes are
  distinct; that is not guaranteed.

## Root cause, measured

From `flow.log` on joe, 2026-10-11:

```
feature match: the menus named 4 film(s) — Jackal | MERCURY | RISING | ALPHA DOG;
               count agreement more(found: 4, expected: 3)
feature match: trying 12 term(s) off the menu text — Jackal | MERCURY | RISING |
               ALPHA DOG | ackal | ckal | Jacka | Jack | ERCURY | RCURY | MERCUR | MERCU
feature match: menu-text terms assigned=false
```

Two faults, both in `OCRSearchTerms.terms`:

1. **Nothing ever joined two lines**, so "MERCURY RISING" was never searched
   for and the menu read as four films against three features.
2. **Shavings of early lines crowded out whole later lines.** Eight of the
   twelve slots were variants of the first two lines, so "ALPHA DOG" — a whole
   line, a real film, printed right there — never got a slot.

## Fixed (partly)

Terms are now ranked by how good the evidence is rather than by which line they
came from, in four tiers, with furniture last in each:

1. whole lines that are not furniture
2. adjacent pairs of those lines joined
3. shavings of those lines
4. everything furniture, whole and shaved

The cap moved 12 → 18. It had to: with whole lines and joins ahead of them,
"CAMPAIGN" — the only term that finds the Get Hard disc's second film, as a
shaving of the damaged line `D°ÇAMPAIGN` — lands at 16, and the headroom is so
the next disc with one more menu line does not silently lose a film.

The corpus caught two wrong attempts at this ordering before the right one, both
by losing Get Hard's second film. That disc is the reason the shavings exist and
it is an unforgiving test of any change to their rank.

## The window, measured and fixed

The suspicion was right and the numbers are exact. Two checks were asking the
same question and giving different answers:

| check | rule | allows at 122 min | Alpha Dog (4.4 min out) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `FeatureMatching.maximumErrorMinutes` | 3 min, flat | 3.0 min | **rejected** |
| `RuntimeCrossCheck` | 6% + 60 s | 8.3 min | accepted |

So the matcher refused to propose a pairing the Start gate would then have
accepted. That is the fault #0216 already fixed once elsewhere, in as many
words: *"two answers to 'do these runtimes agree' is how one report starts
contradicting another."*

`FeatureMatching.fits(runtimeMinutes:titleDurationSeconds:)` now defers to
`RuntimeCrossCheck`, so there is one rule. Proportional also fixes a scale
problem the flat bar always had: three minutes is generous on a 40-minute
episode and tight on a three-hour film, and nothing about TMDB's rounding or a
disc's studio logos scales with runtime the way the tolerance for them should.

A test asserting the old flat bar was rewritten rather than deleted, with the
reason recorded, and two guards added: a film half again as long is still
refused, and the matcher and the cross-check are asserted to agree about Alpha
Dog's real numbers.

## Still open

**(b) The assignment is all-or-nothing.** `assign` returns `nil` unless every
feature fits, so one doubtful film discards the confidently-placed ones beside
it — Jackal (0.24 min out) and Mercury Rising (0.25 min out) were thrown away
with Alpha Dog. Its own doc comment says the honest screen is "two search boxes
with one filled in" and leaves that to the caller; on this disc the caller
filled in none.

With the window fixed, all three of WILLIS's films are inside tolerance, so this
disc should now assign without it. That makes partial assignment a separate
question about a different disc rather than a blocker here — worth doing, but
not to be designed from this one case.

Whether the fix is enough is **unverified on hardware**: it needs that disc, or
another of its shape, in front of the app.

## Relation

`FeatureMatching.splits(from:choose:)` and `MultiTitleInference` already exist
for exactly this and already run on the multi-feature path (`resolveEachFeature`
tries the label, then the model, then the menu text). So this is more likely a
question of why those rungs abstained on this disc than of writing something
new — that is the first thing to find out, before building.

Would make #0245's skip genuinely useful: identify all three, discover The
Jackal is already filed, skip it automatically.
