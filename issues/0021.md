## Description

**The capture is done.** On 2026-09-11 eight DVDs were scanned on the rip host and
15 fixtures were checked in under `ChangeoverTests/Fixtures/makemkvcon/` — seven
movie discs (13 files, 14,175 lines) plus a TV-season disc captured afterwards. The attribute ids used by Changeover are
confirmed against those fixtures and against the MakeMKV.app Info panel, and the
verified table lives in `DiscSelection.md`. What remains open is *structural
coverage*: three disc shapes that would exercise branches no captured disc reaches.

## Long Description

`RemoteControl.md` (lines 266–270) flagged this and the roadmap repeated it
(`Roadmap.md` lines 111–113), because a wrong attribute id does not produce a crash
or a compile error — it produces a title list where "duration" is silently the codec
name, the longest-title heuristic picks a trailer, and the rip looks like it worked.
The experiment in `DiscSelection.md` closed that risk and, more usefully, disproved
four things the plan had asserted from documentation. Those corrections are carried
into [#0023](0023.md), [#0024](0024.md), [#0025](0025.md), [#0027](0027.md),
[#0028](0028.md) and [#0029](0029.md) rather than repeated here.

**`usage.txt` is wrong about MakeMKV's own output format**, which was the first
thing the capture settled. It documents `TINFO:id,code,value` and `TCOUT:count`.
Real output is:

```
TCOUNT:5                                    ← not TCOUT
CINFO:id,code,value
TINFO:title,id,code,value                   ← extra leading field
SINFO:title,stream,id,code,value            ← two extra leading fields
```

Every fixture with titles carries `TCOUNT:`; none carries `TCOUT:`. The earlier
sketch in `RemoteControl.md` lines 235–241 (`TINFO:id,code,value`) is missing the
same fields, so a parser written from either source would read the title index as
the attribute id on every line. **Do not cite `usage.txt` as the authority for the
line shapes** — cite the fixtures.

Within those lines, `id` is the attribute id and `code` is a *message* code used to
look up a localized display name. The roadmap's phrasing "verify the `TINFO`/`SINFO`
numeric attribute codes" reads as though `code` is the interesting field. It is
`id`. In practice `code` is `0` on most attributes and non-zero on the localized
ones — e.g. `SINFO:0,0,1,6201,"Video"`, `SINFO:0,1,1,6202,"Audio"`,
`SINFO:0,6,1,6203,"Subtitles"` in
`ChangeoverTests/Fixtures/makemkvcon/super-troopers-2-min0.txt`, which is a compact
demonstration that attribute 1 must never be branched on.

**Attribute 22 is a bitfield and the most useful stream attribute nobody had
planned for.** Four values occur across the corpus (723 × `0`, 25 × `1`, 23 × `2`,
10 × `4096`):

- `4096` — **forced subtitles**. Every one of the ten occurrences is an
  `S_VOBSUB` stream whose attribute 30 also ends `(forced only)`, e.g.
  `SINFO:0,6,22,0,"4096"` with `SINFO:0,6,30,0," English  (forced only)"` in
  `super-troopers-2-min0.txt`. Detect forced subs from attribute 22, **not** by
  string-matching attribute 30.
- `1` / `2` — **commentary**, on audio streams. `supertroopers-min0.txt` title 0
  streams 4 and 5 are both `DD Stereo English` 192 Kb/s and differ only in
  attribute 22 (`1` and `2`) — a cast commentary and a director commentary.
  `hanna-min0.txt` title 3 stream 5 carries `22 = "1"` where stream 4 carries `0`,
  and the two are otherwise byte-identical in every other attribute. This is a
  partial answer to the commentary problem [#0027](0027.md) inherits; see that
  ticket for where it stops working.

## Expected behavior

A checked-in reference table mapping each attribute id used by Changeover to its

## Actual behavior

The table above and 15 fixtures now exist, so a parser can be written and tested
with no drive. Three disc shapes are still uncaptured, and each one is the only
evidence for a branch the design now depends on:

1. **A disc with two feature-length titles** — theatrical plus extended cut, or
   seamless branching. This is the 2+ fallback in [#0025](0025.md) and
   [#0026](0026.md), and it occurred on **none** of the captured discs. A
   director's-cut edition is the likeliest source. **This is now the only untested
   branch.**
2. **A Disney/Pixar disc** — the canonical structural-obfuscation case, and the
   one most likely to falsify "exactly one title ≥ 45 min". Note that animated
   features run ~70 min, so an animated disc also probes the threshold's margin.

**A TV-season disc was captured, and it falsified the assumption this list used to
carry.** The expectation was that a TV disc would yield *zero* titles ≥ 45 min and
identify itself that way. Brooklyn Nine-Nine does the opposite: it yields exactly
one — title 12, 2:53:18, 33 chapters, 6.9 GB — and it is a "Play All" concatenation
of eight ~21-minute episodes. Largest-title, most-chapters and exactly-one-over-45
all agree and are all wrong together. Without the Play All guard now specified in
[#0025](0025.md), the app would have filed a season of television in the Plex Movies
library as a feature film, silently. This is the single most valuable capture in the
corpus.

Worth noting what the margin actually is: the longest non-feature title in the whole
corpus is `super-troopers-2-min0.txt` title 30 at **0:39:46** (a 1.4 GB
behind-the-scenes piece with 5 chapters). The 45-minute threshold clears the
observed worst case by only 5 minutes 14 seconds, which is the strongest argument
for capturing an animated feature before treating the threshold as settled.

## Notes

`/opt/homebrew/bin/makemkvcon` nor `/opt/homebrew/bin/HandBrakeCLI` — the defaults

## Plan

### 1. Capture fixtures — **done**

`Tools/capture-disc.sh` runs both scans per disc on the rip host and saves the
fixtures. Seven discs are captured; the corpus and what each one demonstrates is
tabled above. Fixtures live in `ChangeoverTests/Fixtures/makemkvcon/` — that is
their permanent home, not `issues/0021/`, because [#0023](0023.md) runs its tests
against them directly. Nothing is scrubbed except that the drive serial does appear
in the `DRV` and `MSG:2010` lines and was left as-is.

Three discs remain wanted, listed under `## Actual behavior`. Capture them with the
same tool when they turn up; each one only adds fixtures, it does not change
anything already confirmed.

### 2. Build the attribute table — **done**

The verified table is above and in `DiscSelection.md`. Corrections against the
hypotheses this ticket was filed with:

- **`TCOUNT:`, not `TCOUT:`.** `usage.txt` is wrong; every fixture says `TCOUNT`.
- **Attribute 27 is confirmed and present on every title**, but its value is *not*
  `title_t00.mkv`. It is `<letter><digit>_t<NN>.mkv` (`B1_t00.mkv`,
  `D1_t01.mkv`), optionally prefixed with the disc name
  (`Girl in the Spider's Web, The-B1_t00.mkv`). Whether the prefix appears varies
  by disc, not by scan. **And it is not unique across scans of the same disc** —
  see [#0028](0028.md), which no longer verifies a rip by filename.
- **Attribute 16 (SourceFileName) does not appear** on any title in any fixture.
  `DiscTitle.sourceFileName` in [#0022](0022.md) will always be `nil` on DVDs.
- **Attribute 15 (AngleInfo) never appears** on any title, and **attribute 33
  (OrderWeight) is always `"0"`** — neither is usable as a signal.
- **`CINFO` lines do exist and are short** — six to eight per disc, shaped
  `CINFO:id,code,value` exactly as documented. Attribute **32 is the volume name**
  (`CINFO:32,0,"WEIRD_SCIENCE"`), and attributes 2, 30 and 32 carry the same
  string. The `DRV:0` line's sixth field carries it too
  (`DRV:0,2,999,1,"…","WEIRD_SCIENCE","/dev/rdisk6"`), so either source works and
  the two agreed on all eight discs.
- **Attributes 31, 40, 42 exist and were not in the hypothesis table.** 31 is an
  HTML tooltip fragment, 40 a channel layout, 42 a conversion note. None is needed;
  they matter only as proof that an unknown attribute id must be ignored rather
  than treated as an error.
- **Attribute 1 (Type) and 4 (LangName) are localized**, confirmed by the non-zero
  `code` field on those lines. Classify streams from attribute 5 (CodecId).

Promote the table into a doc comment above the attribute constants in the parser
when [#0023](0023.md) lands, so the magic numbers and their evidence sit together.

### 3. Nail the lexical details — **done**

- **Quoting.** All string values are quoted. No fixture contains an embedded quote,
  so the escape convention is **still unknown** — do not guess one. A parser should
  handle a doubled `""` and a backslash-escaped `\"` tolerantly and never crash on
  either. Note the `MSG:2010` lines do contain `\"` inside the quoted message text
  (`"Optical drive \"DVD+R-DL …\" opened in OS access mode."`), which is the closest
  evidence available and points at backslash escaping.
- **Commas inside quoted values.** Confirmed and unavoidable. `TINFO:0,27,0,`
  `"Girl in the Spider's Web, The-B1_t00.mkv"` in
  `the-girl-in-the-spider-s-web-min0.txt` contains a comma inside the quoted value,
  as does attribute 30 on the same title. A naive `split(separator: ",")` gets that
  disc wrong. Apostrophes appear in the same value; they are not special.
- **Colons inside quoted values.** Duration (`"1:55:11"`) and frame rate
  (`"29.97 (30000/1001)"`) mean the line-prefix split must be on the *first* colon
  only.
- **Line termination.** The captures are `\n`-terminated throughout. `PRGV`/`PRGC`/
  `PRGT` lines do not appear in `info` output at all, so progress parsing is a rip-
  time concern, not a scan-time one. The carry-over-buffer fix in [#0024](0024.md)
  is still required — it is a property of `availableData`, not of the fixtures.
- **`MSG` lines.** The shape is
  `MSG:code,flags,paramCount,message,format,params…`, with the params repeating
  the `%1`/`%2` substitutions as separate trailing fields. This is what makes
  `MSG:3025` readable rather than guessable: its params are
  `"1","23","120"` — title, actual length, **effective minimum length**. Codes
  observed across the corpus: `1005` started, `1009` profile parse error, `2003`
  scsi/scramble error, `2010` drive opened, `3007` direct disc access, `3010` cell
  lookup failure, `3025` title below minimum length, `3026` fake title,
  `3027` duplicate title skipped, `3028` title added, `3038` cells removed from
  title end, `4004` source file corrupt, `5011` operation successfully completed,
  `5021` key expired / version too old.
- **Exit status.** Only one exit status was recorded as a fixture:
  `keyexpired-v1.18.3-exit253.txt` (exit **253**, `MSG:5021`, no `TCOUNT` line).
  Every other capture exited 0 — including the two discs with real read errors.
  **This is thin evidence and [#0024](0024.md) should say so**: the fixture corpus
  proves that error text does not imply failure, but it contains exactly one
  failing run. A no-disc capture and a second key-expiry capture on the current
  MakeMKV version are both cheap and still missing.
- **`TCOUNT` versus title count.** They agree on all 12 fixtures that have titles
  (e.g. `TCOUNT:26` and title indices 0–25 in
  `the-girl-in-the-spider-s-web-min0.txt`). The parser should still trust the
  `TINFO` lines and log a discrepancy rather than trusting the count.

### 4. What not to do

Do not add a MakeMKV SDK dependency or vendor `apdefs.h` into the repo to shortcut
this. The SDK carries its own licensing, the app shells out to the CLI by design
(`Changeover/RipController.swift:17-24`), and a confirmed table plus fixtures is a
smaller and more durable artifact than a header file the build would have to track.

Do not treat `usage.txt` as authoritative for anything the fixtures cover. It is
wrong about `TCOUT`/`TCOUNT` and about the `TINFO`/`SINFO` field counts.

No Swift source changes land in this ticket — it produces fixtures, a table, and the
notes the rest of the phase is written from.

## Re-triage

**2026-09-11 — obsolete under the MakeMKV replacement. Recommend dropping.** Per
`MakeMKVReplacement-Path.md` §4, which lists this ticket as the only one made
outright obsolete by the decision. The status transition is the user's call, so
this stays `open` until they say otherwise.

`makemkvcon` robot-mode attribute ids are **no longer parsed at all**. HandBrake
becomes the scanner — `HandBrakeCLI --scan --title 0 --min-duration 1 --json`,
verified on *Fargo* as returning `MainFeature: 1` across 8 titles
(`MakeMKVReplacement-Results.md` §2) — and [#0023](0023.md)'s robot-mode line
parser is being rewritten against that JSON rather than against `TINFO`/`SINFO`.
With no parser reading attribute ids, there is nothing for a verified attribute
table to be verified *for*.

**In fairness: this ticket was tractable, and it was nearly done.**
`MakeMKVReplacement-Findings.md` §2.1 established that `apdefs.h` ships in the
`makemkv-oss` tarball at `makemkvgui/inc/lgpl/apdefs.h`, is public domain, and is
frozen — so the premise that the attribute table was an undocumented moving target
was itself wrong. On top of that, the capture described above is *done*: 15
fixtures across eight discs, 14,175 lines, checked in under
`ChangeoverTests/Fixtures/makemkvcon/`, with the table confirmed against both the
fixtures and the MakeMKV.app Info panel. What remained was structural coverage of
three disc shapes.

So this is being dropped **because the parser it serves is being dropped, not
because it was unworkable.** That distinction matters if the decision is ever
revisited: the work product — the fixtures, the verified table in
`DiscSelection.md`, the `TCOUNT`/`TINFO`/`SINFO` field-count corrections — stays
valid and stays checked in. [#0015](0015.md) keeps `makemkvcon` as an optional
fallback ripper, and if that fallback ever needs to *scan* rather than just rip,
everything here is still good.

Three findings in this ticket are not MakeMKV-specific and should survive into the
HandBrake parser in [#0023](0023.md): a wrong field offset produces a plausible
wrong answer rather than an error; the tool's own published documentation was
wrong about its own output format; and the only way to know is to capture real
output and test against it. HandBrake's `--json` has its own version of the third
problem — `MakeMKVReplacement-Results.md` §10 documents that the output is not a
single JSON document and carries interleaved libdvdnav log lines on stdout.
