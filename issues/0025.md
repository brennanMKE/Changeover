## Description

A scanned disc yields a flat list of titles with no opinion about which one is the
movie. The disc-selection experiment (`DiscSelection.md`, fixtures in
`ChangeoverTests/Fixtures/makemkvcon/`) settled what that opinion should be, and it
is much smaller than this ticket was originally written for: **scan once at an
explicit `--minlength`, and the movie is the only title running 45 minutes or
longer.** That holds on 8 of 9 discs.

**The ninth disc is why the rule cannot ship alone.** A Brooklyn Nine-Nine season
disc satisfies the rule perfectly and gives the wrong answer: its single ≥45-minute
title is a "Play All" concatenation of eight sitcom episodes. A **Play All guard**
— a pure comparison over durations — is therefore part of this ticket, not an
enhancement to it. The scoring engine this ticket used to describe is still not
needed; the fallbacks for "zero such titles" and "two or more", plus the guard, now
carry the whole design.

## Root cause

Nothing decided which scanned title was the movie. The Re-triage resolved most of
this by adopting HandBrake's own `MainFeature` scan field as the answer (verified
correct on Fargo: index 1 among 8), leaving only the one thing `MainFeature` cannot
provide: refusing a TV-season "Play All" title, which is the longest, largest, and
most-chaptered title on a season disc and would otherwise be filed into the Plex
Movies library as a feature film, confidently and silently.

A previous session wrote `DiscTitleHeuristic.swift` and its tests to this design but
never ran them (the test host was down), leaving three real defects in the
uncommitted work, found on review:

1. The `superTroopers2IsAMovieNotAPlayAll` fixture built its feature title at index
   `0` but called `classify(mainFeatureIndex: 1)`. HandBrake indices are 1-based, so
   `classify` looked up an index the disc didn't have and fell through to `.none` —
   the test was passing (before it was ever run) for the wrong reason, and the
   negative-result assertion it exists to protect (that summing the whole
   non-feature pool is dangerous, not just clustering) was never actually exercised.
2. `playAllEpisodes` compared durations through `Double` (`seed.durationSeconds) *
   playAllEpisodeSimilarity`, `Int(playAllDurationTolerance * 100)`) where the Plan
   calls for integer arithmetic throughout ("do not compare `Duration` values or
   convert through `Double`").
3. `applyingSuggestedRoles` reimplemented its own "is this the main feature" check
   from `mainFeatureIndex` directly, instead of being built on `classify` as the Plan
   specifies. That meant a `.playAll` outcome from `classify` (refuse to call it a
   movie) and `applyingSuggestedRoles` (used to populate `DiscTitle.suggestedRole`
   for the UI) could disagree: the Play All title would still be silently marked
   `.mainFeature` for the UI even though `classify` had correctly refused it — the
   exact "confidently and silently" failure this ticket exists to prevent, just moved
   one function over.

## Fix

- Fixed the Super Troopers 2 fixture to use HandBrake's 1-based indexing

## Long Description

The current app has a heuristic, just a much worse one and in the wrong place:
`largestMKV(in:)` (`Changeover/RipController.swift:66-77`) picks the biggest file in
the working directory *after* ripping everything. Moving the decision before the rip
is what converts the guess into a choice.

### What the fixtures showed

Every disc carries **exactly one** title of 45 minutes or more, out of 5 to 35
titles total:

| disc | titles (`min0`) | ≥45 min | main idx | chapters | duration | correct? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weird Science | 11 | **1** | 0 | 18 | 1:33:22 | yes |
| The Girl With The Dragon Tattoo | 5 | **1** | 0 | 16 | 2:37:51 | yes |
| The Girl in the Spider's Web | 26 | **1** | 0 | 16 | 1:55:11 | yes |
| Hornets' Nest | 10 | **1** | **9** | 16 | 2:26:47 | yes |
| Hanna | 16 | **1** | **3** | 21 | 1:50:45 | yes |
| Super Troopers | 22 | **1** | 0 | 21 | 1:39:47 | yes |
| Super Troopers 2 | 35 | **1** | 0 | 25 | 1:39:15 | yes |
| **Brooklyn Nine-Nine** (TV season) | **30** | **1** | **12** | **33** | **2:53:18** | **NO — Play All** |

### Scorecard

| rule | score | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **exactly one title ≥ 45 min** | **8/9** | adopt **only with the Play All guard** |
| largest title by `sizeBytes` | 8/9 | fails on the same disc, for the same reason |
| most chapters | 8/9 | fails on the same disc (33 chapters — 8 episodes × 4, plus one) |
| duration matches TMDB runtime | **9/9** | promoted: it is the independent safety net, not a tiebreaker |
| **first title (index 0)** | 5/9 | never assume — Hanna is index 3, Hornets' Nest 9, Brooklyn 12 |

The three duration/size/chapter rules are not independent. On Brooklyn Nine-Nine
they agree with each other and are **all wrong together**, which is precisely why a
cross-check from a different source (TMDB runtime) matters more than a fourth
structural heuristic would.

The margin is real but not enormous. The longest non-feature title in the corpus is
`super-troopers-2-min0.txt` title 30 at **0:39:46** — a 1.4 GB behind-the-scenes
piece. 45 minutes clears the observed worst case by 5:14. The threshold should be a
named constant with that evidence in a comment, not a magic number.

### The Play All guard

Some TV-season discs author a "Play All" title that concatenates every episode. It
is longer than 45 minutes, it is the largest title, and it has the most chapters, so
every structural rule points at it. Left unguarded, the app would rip 6.9 GB of
television and file it in the Plex **Movies** library as a feature film —
confidently, silently, and passing every other check. That is the worst failure
available in this phase; it is also the one the user would be least likely to notice
until Plex showed it.

The guard, verified against all nine fixtures:

1. **Discard titles under 5 minutes.** Stubs, menu loops and the 5-second decoys
   that fill `the-girl-in-the-spider-s-web-min0.txt`.
2. **Of the remainder (excluding the long title), find the largest cluster whose
   durations are all within 15% of a seed member.**
3. **If that cluster has ≥ 3 members *and* its total duration is within 2% of the
   long title's duration, the long title is a Play All.** Report "this looks like a
   TV disc" instead of returning a movie.

The separation is total. No threshold tuning was needed:

| disc | cluster n | cluster sum | feature | error | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **brooklyn-nine-nine** | **8** | **10,398 s** | **10,398 s** | **0.0%** | **TV disc** |
| dragon-tattoo | 0 | 0 | 9,471 s | 100.0% | movie |
| hanna | 1 | 424 s | 6,645 s | 93.6% | movie |
| hornets-nest | 2 | 1,088 s | 8,807 s | 87.6% | movie |
| super-troopers-2 | 1 | 1,170 s | 5,955 s | 80.4% | movie |
| supertroopers | 1 | 368 s | 5,987 s | 93.9% | movie |
| spider-s-web | 1 | 590 s | 6,911 s | 91.5% | movie |
| weird-science | 2 | 726 s | 5,602 s | 87.0% | movie |

The nearest movie to the boundary misses by 80 percentage points. The eight
Brooklyn episodes (titles 13–20, 20:55 to 22:45) sum to **exactly** the Play All
duration, to the second.

**Negative result — do not re-derive it.** Summing *all* other titles instead of
clustering does **not** work. Super Troopers 2's non-feature titles sum to 5,390 s
against a 5,955 s feature — a ratio of **0.905**, uncomfortably close to a false
positive, and it would only take one more bonus featurette to cross any tolerance
loose enough to be useful. The clustering step is what makes the guard safe: it
requires the other titles to be *similar to each other*, which is what "episodes"
means and what "a trailer, a featurette and a deleted scene" does not. Write this
down in the code comment, not just here.

**Also checked and rejected: segment count (attribute 25).** Brooklyn's Play All
title has `TINFO:12,25,0,"9"` and a segment map of
`"1-4,5-8,9-12,13-16,17-20,21-24,25-28,29-32,33"`, which looks like a perfect
signal — until Super Troopers' feature turns out to have segment count **12**.
Movies in the corpus range from 1 to 12 segments. It is not usable.

### No LLM is warranted, and that is worth writing down

The experiment existed partly to test whether a local model (LM Studio) should score
the title list. It should not. The problem has **one numeric feature and a
threshold**; a comparison resolves it exactly. A local model would be
non-deterministic across runs on identical input, slower by orders of magnitude, and
able to hallucinate a title index that does not exist — and a wrong index here means
ripping the wrong thing for forty minutes. The fixtures are available if someone
wants to evaluate a model offline out of curiosity; nothing about that belongs in
the app.

### Why the TMDB runtime check still earns its place

Brooklyn Nine-Nine promoted this from "nice tiebreaker" to **the only independent
check in the system**. Every structural rule reads the same disc metadata and can be
fooled by the same disc; TMDB is a second source. On that disc it rejects the answer
outright — no movie runs 173 minutes *and* matches a sitcom title search — which
means it would catch the failure even if the Play All guard were somehow evaded.
Treat a large runtime mismatch as a reason to stop and ask, not as a tiebreak input.

Beyond that safety-net role it does two things the threshold cannot:

- **Disambiguates remakes.** *The Girl With The Dragon Tattoo* exists as a 2009
  Swedish film (152 min) and a 2011 Fincher film (158 min). `dragon-tattoo-min0.txt`
  title 0 runs 2:37:51 — 157.9 min — which identifies *which film the disc is*, not
  merely which title. That is the same ambiguity the `{tmdb-ID}` folder convention
  exists to resolve, answered from the same signal.
- **Breaks the 2+ tie.** Theatrical versus extended cut is a runtime difference and
  nothing else.

Two constraints on using it, both of which are why it is [#0032](0032.md) rather
than a line in this ticket:

- **`TMDBClient` cannot supply a runtime today.** `searchMovies(query:apiKey:)`
  calls `/search/movie` (`Changeover/TMDB/TMDBClient.swift:18`), and that endpoint
  does not return `runtime`. A `/movie/{id}` fetch is required, which is a new
  request, a new response model and a new failure mode.
- **PAL transfers run ~4% fast.** A PAL DVD of a 100-minute film runs about 96
  minutes. Any runtime comparison needs a tolerance that accommodates that, which
  means the tolerance is wide enough that the check is a *tiebreaker and a sanity
  check*, never a primary selector.

## Expected behavior

Given a parsed `DiscInfo` from a scan at a known `--minlength`, count the titles at
or above the feature threshold:

- **Exactly one, and the Play All guard does not fire** — mark it `.mainFeature`.
  Everything else is `.extra`. This is the case on all seven movie discs and it
  needs no user interaction.
- **Exactly one, but the Play All guard fires** — do **not** return a movie. Report
  a probable TV-season disc, naming the episode cluster it found ("8 titles of
  ~21 minutes summing to the length of title 12"). TV support is out of scope for
  Phase 2 (`Roadmap.md` line 307), so the outcome is an honest refusal, not a rip.
  The user can still override via the picker; the default must not be "rip it as a
  movie".
- **Zero** — do not promote anything. This is not a movie disc, or it is a very
  short feature, or it is a TV-season disc **with no Play All title** — which was
  the assumed shape of a TV disc before Brooklyn Nine-Nine was captured, and is
  presumably still a real shape. Hand it to the user; [#0026](0026.md) shows the
  full picker and says why.
- **Two or more** — a theatrical/extended pair, seamless branching, or a double
  feature. Rank the candidates by closeness to the TMDB runtime
  ([#0032](0032.md)), preselect the closest, and **still show the picker** —
  do not decide silently. **This case never occurred in the nine captured discs
  and is entirely untested.** [#0021](0021.md) wants a disc that produces it.

The result is deterministic: re-running on the same input gives the same answer.

## Actual behavior

No such function exists. The closest thing is `largestMKV(in:)`

## Plan

### Files

### Shape

```swift
nonisolated enum DiscTitleHeuristic {
    enum Outcome {
        case single(index: Int)           // exactly one candidate — no interaction
        case playAll(index: Int, episodes: [Int])  // TV season disc — refuse, explain
        case none(candidates: [Int])      // nothing clears the bar — ask
        case ambiguous(candidates: [Int]) // 2+ — rank by runtime, then ask
    }

    static func classify(
        _ disc: DiscInfo,
        featureMinimumSeconds: Int = 45 * 60
    ) -> Outcome

    /// Pure, testable on its own: does `candidate` look like a Play All title?
    /// Returns the episode cluster it found, or nil.
    static func playAllEpisodes(
        for candidate: DiscTitle,
        among titles: [DiscTitle]
    ) -> [DiscTitle]?

    /// Returns a copy of `disc` with `suggestedRole` assigned on every title.
    static func applyingSuggestedRoles(
        to disc: DiscInfo,
        featureMinimumSeconds: Int = 45 * 60
    ) -> DiscInfo
}
```

`nonisolated` because [#0024](0024.md) applies this immediately after parsing,
inside a `nonisolated static` scan — under
`SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION = MainActor`
(`Changeover.xcodeproj/project.pbxproj:416,445`) the default would be `@MainActor`
and unreachable from there.

Returning an `Outcome` rather than just a mutated `DiscInfo` is the change the
experiment forces. The four cases are genuinely different products — "here is your
movie", "this is a TV disc and I will not guess", "I do not know, look at this",
"it is one of these two" — and [#0026](0026.md) renders each differently.
Collapsing them into "there is always a `.mainFeature`" is what would put a trailer
in the Plex library, and collapsing `.playAll` into `.single` is what would put a
whole season of television there.

Keep `applyingSuggestedRoles` as the role-assignment half so `DiscTitle.suggestedRole`
is populated for the UI; implement it on top of `classify`.

### Algorithm

1. Candidates are the titles with `durationSeconds >= featureMinimumSeconds`.
   That is the whole selection rule.
2. One candidate → run `playAllEpisodes(for:among:)` on it. A non-nil result →
   `.playAll`. Otherwise `.single`: it is `.mainFeature`; every other title is
   `.extra`.

   The guard itself, in integer arithmetic:

   ```
   pool     = titles.filter { $0.index != candidate.index && $0.durationSeconds >= 300 }
   cluster  = largest subset of pool whose durations are all within 15% of some seed
   isPlayAll = cluster.count >= 3
            && abs(cluster.totalSeconds - candidate.durationSeconds) * 100
                 <= 2 * candidate.durationSeconds
   ```

   Both thresholds (15%, 2%) sit in the middle of an enormous gap — see the table
   above — so they are not tuned parameters and should not be exposed as settings.
   Name them as constants with the measured separation in a comment.
3. Zero candidates → `.none`. Do **not** promote the longest title present. Every
   title is `.extra`. [#0026](0026.md) shows the picker and says the disc did not
   identify itself.
4. Two or more → `.ambiguous`, candidates sorted by descending duration so the
   caller has a stable order even with no TMDB runtime available.

No scoring, no weights, no near-duplicate window, no `nearDuplicateSeconds`
constant. The old plan's near-tie tiebreak (chapter count, then size, then lowest
index) is **deleted**, not moved: two candidates within seconds of each other is
exactly the case that must reach the user rather than be resolved by a rule that
lands differently per disc. Chapter count and size stay in the UI as columns
([#0026](0026.md)) and as a cross-check in tests — both scored 8/9, failing only
where the duration rule fails — but they are
not inputs to the decision.

Keep the comparison in integer seconds. Do not compare `Duration` values or convert
through `Double`.

### The `.ignore` role

`DiscTitle.Role` from [#0022](0022.md) has `mainFeature`, `extra`, `ignore`.
With extras now having a real destination ([#0031](0031.md)), `.ignore` means "not
worth keeping at all" rather than "shorter than the threshold" — menu loops, FBI
warnings, the 5-second fake titles that fill
`the-girl-in-the-spider-s-web-min0.txt`. A short-duration cutoff for `.ignore` is
reasonable (the old 300-second default is a sane starting point) but it is a
*presentation* threshold, not a selection one, and it must never hide a title from
the list — [#0026](0026.md) collapses them behind a disclosure instead, because the
phase's exit criterion is that the title list matches what MakeMKV.app shows.

Note that MakeMKV already filters some of this upstream: `MSG:3026` ("assuming fake
title") and `MSG:3027` ("is equal to title #N and was skipped") mean the decoy and
duplicate detection is done before the app ever sees the list. Do not reimplement
it.

### Settings

Two integers in `Changeover/AppSettings.swift`, alongside the existing stored
properties (`:13-16`), with `Keys` entries (`:58-63`), loads in `init()` (`:38-43`)
and writes in `persist()` (`:48-54`):

```swift
var scanMinLengthSeconds: Int = 0        // passed to BOTH scan and rip — see #0028
var featureMinimumSeconds: Int = 45 * 60
```

`scanMinLengthSeconds` belongs to [#0024](0024.md) and [#0028](0028.md); it is
named here because the two thresholds are easy to confuse and must not be the same
setting. The scan threshold decides *what MakeMKV lists and how it numbers it*; the
feature threshold decides *which of those is the movie*. Conflating them is how the
index-shift bug in [#0028](0028.md) gets reintroduced.

The existing `init()` guards every load with `!v.isEmpty`; an `Int` needs a
different guard, since `UserDefaults.integer` returns 0 for a missing key — and for
`scanMinLengthSeconds` **0 is a legitimate value**, so `object(forKey:) as? Int` is
required, not optional style preference.

### Explicitly out of scope

Do not try to identify *which* extras are alternate angles, commentaries or
episodes. Attribute 15 (AngleInfo) does not appear on any captured disc and
attribute 25 (SegmentsCount) is suggestive at best. Surface them in the UI as
columns if useful; do not branch the default on them.
