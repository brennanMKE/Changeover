## Description

A third WILLIS collection holds three films:

| title | runtime | chapters | film |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1:46:56 | 16 | Sunset (1988) |
| 4 | 1:39:49 | 11 | Hudson Hawk (1991) |
| 8 | 1:38:30 | 9 | The Whole Nine Yards (2000) |

Titles 4 and 8 are **79 seconds apart**. `featureGroups` single-links any two
titles within `distinctFeatureSeparationSeconds` (5 minutes), so two entirely
different films were chained into one group, the disc was read as holding **two**
features, and The Whole Nine Yards could not be imported at all.

The menu named all three — `SUNSET | HUDSON HAWK | The- | Whole | Nine Yards` —
and `flow.log` recorded the disagreement before the grouping won anyway:

```
feature resolve: 2 features, 2 scanned title(s)
feature match: the menus named 3 film(s) — SUNSET | HUDSON HAWK | NINE YARDS;
               count agreement more(found: 3, expected: 2)
```

The app had the right answer in hand, said so in its own log, and discarded it.

## Root cause

The proximity rule assumes two different films are minutes apart. That is a
reasonable prior and this disc falsifies it with real data: two films from
different studios, twelve years apart, happen to run within 79 seconds of each
other.

## Fix

Two titles merge into one group only when they are close in runtime **and share
a chapter count**. The evidence is unanimous across everything this project has:

    Adventureland   8 branching variants   29 chapters each   ONE film
    Identity        two cuts, 67 s apart   28 and 28          ONE film
    Repo Men        two cuts, 8 min apart  21 and 21          ONE film
    Hudson Hawk / The Whole Nine Yards     11 and 9           TWO films

Two cuts of one film are authored from one chapter list — an extended cut adds
footage inside existing chapters, and a branching variant is the same cells in a
different order. Two films have no reason to agree.

## What is deliberately not claimed

**Agreeing counts are not evidence of one film.** Uncle Buck and The Great
Outdoors are two films with 17 chapters each, and #0157's removed rule failed by
assuming otherwise. This rule only ever *separates*; it never joins. Pinned by
`agreeingChapterCountsDoNotJoinTwoFilms`.

**A count of zero is unknown, not different.** A scan that could not read a
chapter list must not split a film in two; the pair falls back to proximity
alone. Pinned by `anunknownChapterCountDoesNotSplitAFilm`.

## Relation

The mirror image of #0235, and the pair is worth seeing together: there, two
cuts looked like two films and only the menu could tell; here, two films looked
like two cuts and only the chapters could. Runtime proximity alone decides
neither.

Helps #0246 directly — the disc now presents three features to identify instead
of two.
