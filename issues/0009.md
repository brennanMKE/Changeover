## Description

When a rip fails, the last thing the log says is `"✗ makemkvcon exited with status 1"` (`RipController.swift:50-51`), by which time the tool's own explanation has scrolled past in a 130-point-tall log view. An expired MakeMKV key, a disc the drive cannot read, and no disc in the tray are all reported identically. `EncodeController` has the same shape (`EncodeController.swift:50-52`).

## Resolution notes

> 🟢 Resolved 2026-09-12 — HandBrakeCLI failures are now drained completely (`ProcessRunner`), classified into a `FailureReason` from real 1.11.2 captures (`HandBrakeFailureClassifier`), and presented as an actionable headline plus details in the log and a banner (`FailurePresenter`), with tool-shaped failures never triggering the MakeMKV fallback.
>
> **Reviewer's independent run (2026-09-12, `./run-remote-tests.sh gordon`, full sync):** `✔ Test run with 163 tests in 15 suites passed after 24.329 seconds`, `** TEST SUCCEEDED **`, 0 `CONTINUATION MISUSE` matches in the full log. Key timings agree with the draft: `aLongHealthyRunIsNeverBoundedByTheGracePeriod()` 6.204s (the full ~6s healthy workload), `runReturnsWithinTheHardCeilingWhenAnOrphanKeepsThePipeOpen()` 1.038s, `encodeSurvivesTheReaderTerminationRaceAndKeepsTheFinalLine()` 7.727s, `readerDoneAloneNeverArmsGraceOrFires()` 0.509s, and all three `runMakeMKV…` race regressions passed unmodified.
>
> **Static review of `ProcessRunner.run` / `RunCompletionGate`:** the continuation resumes exactly once on every path (normal exit, either ordering of reader-EOF and process-exit, watchdog timeout, `Process.run()` throwing via `abandon()`, and an orphan holding the pipe via grace). The gate self-retains until it fires; grace arms only from `markProcessDone(_:)`, so nothing but the watchdog can stop a healthy, still-producing process; the `process ↔ gate` cycle is broken in `onReady` and in the launch-failure path; the captured `absoluteWorkItem`/`inactivityTimer` vars are only ever touched after the gate's lock has ordered `terminationHandler`'s reads before `onReady`'s writes (the project builds in Swift 5 mode, which is why the captures compile). Classifier asymmetry holds: `.toolIncompatible` is not disc-shaped, unmatched output stays `.toolExited` (disc-shaped), and `outputOpenFailed` is path-anchored.
>
> **Non-blocking findings, recorded for a follow-up rather than a bounce:**
> 1. `ProcessRunner.swift:178-179` — on the grace-expiry path only, `onReady` nils `readabilityHandler` and closes the read handle while the reader's dispatch source may still be live; an orphan writing in that exact window could hit `availableData` on a closed descriptor. The normal path is safe (the reader nils itself before `markReaderDone()`). Consider skipping the explicit read-side `close()` there and letting the `Pipe` close it on dealloc.
> 2. `ProcessRunner.swift:377-379` vs `:350-361` — if `markReaderDone()` fires the gate between `markProcessDone` releasing its lock and `armGrace()` storing the timer, the grace item is scheduled after the fire; it is a guarded no-op, but holds the gate (and `process`/`pipe` via `onReady`) for up to `hardCeilingGrace` seconds. Harmless.
> 3. `FailurePresenter.swift:33-37` — "stopped unexpectedly (signal N)" is chosen from `logTail.last != "HandBrake has exited."`, since `JobFailure` doesn't carry `uncaughtSignal`; an unmatched non-signal exit whose tail lacks the sign-off would read as a signal. Cosmetic, per plan §2.5's wording.
> 4. `HandBrakeFailureClassifier.swift:190,197` — `unrecognized option`, `invalid option` and `Invalid audio encoder` ship as unconfirmed analogues of confirmed non-disc rows, a slight departure from §2.3's letter. Argument-parsing text can't plausibly come from disc reading and test 8 proves none appears in the real success capture; accepted.
> 5. `FailurePresenter` renders `.toolIncompatible`'s quoted line twice ("It said:" in the first detail and "HandBrake said:" as evidence). Cosmetic.
> 6. `ChangeoverTests/ProcessRunnerTests.swift:214-217` — the suite comment still says the three backstop claims "have not been confirmed by an actual run"; they now have. Code-only comment, left for the next code commit that touches the file.
>
> **Script note:** `run-remote-tests.sh`'s "-- failures (first 30):" section printed benign `[Connection] … com.apple.linkd.autoShortcut` lines on this passing run — its failure grep is broader than it should be. Reported, not patched.

## Root cause

`EncodeController`'s reader streamed HandBrakeCLI's output into the log and a
40-line tail buffer, then discarded it once the process exited — a non-zero
exit always became the same undifferentiated `.toolExited(code:)`, and the
reader itself had the same trailing-output race #0015 found and fixed in
`MakeMKVRipper.runMakeMKV` (`terminationHandler` disabled the reader with no
final drain), plus no carry-over between chunks and a dropped chunk whenever
a chunk boundary split a multi-byte UTF-8 character. Nothing turned tool
output, or even the exit code, into an explanation a person could act on.

## Fix

Followed the revised `## Plan` below. Two commits, per §7, plus one
addendum:

- **Commit A (`6143729`)** — extracted `Changeover/ProcessRunner.swift` from
  `MakeMKVRipper.runMakeMKV`'s reader/termination-race fix: one reader
  (the readability handler), `RunCompletionGate` resuming only after both
  pipe EOF and process exit, and a byte-level `LineSplitter` that never
  drops a chunk split inside a UTF-8 sequence and splits on `\r` as well as
  `\n` (HandBrake interleaves `\r` progress with `\n` log lines on one
  pipe). `runMakeMKV` keeps its exact signature and delegates to
  `ProcessRunner.run(watchdog: .absolute(hangTimeout), …)`, unchanged
  behaviour. `EncodeController.encode` migrates onto the same runner with an
  **inactivity** watchdog (30 min of no output) instead of an absolute one —
  #0018 measured a 40m06s real encode, so an absolute bound would risk
  killing a healthy job. No classifier yet at this commit: a non-zero exit
  is still `.toolExited(code:)`.
- **Commit B (`0f35f89`)** — `HandBrakeFailureClassifier` (pure, tiered
  substring matching over `ProcessRunner.Termination` + streamed lines) and
  `FailurePresenter` (headline + details, never `String(describing:)`).
  Added `FailureReason.toolIncompatible(detail:)` for HandBrakeCLI rejecting
  Changeover's own arguments, wired as *not* disc-shaped in
  `FallbackPolicy.isDiscShaped`. `EncodeController` now builds a
  `HandBrakeFailureClassifier.Input` after the process exits (a 40-line
  non-progress tail plus up to 5 evidence lines, per §3) and returns the
  classified reason. `DVDPipeline.finish(_:)` logs the presenter's headline
  and details for every failure; `MetadataEntryView` gets a `failureBanner`
  bound to `jobs.lastOutcome`. Captured real HandBrakeCLI 1.11.2 failures on
  `joe` (C1–C6) rather than shipping the filing's guesses untested — see
  `## Gotchas` for what the captures corrected.
- **Addendum (`34b6940`)** — the plan's §6 tests 14–15 (a classified
  disc-shaped failure still falls back; a classified non-disc failure never
  does) were missing from commit B. Added as a separate commit rather than
  amending B, per this project's "always create new commits" rule.
- **Three more commits, prompted by a real 58-minute hang on gordon during
  verification** (a `SWIFT TASK CONTINUATION MISUSE` against
  `ProcessRunner.run`), each fixing a problem in the previous one before any
  test run confirmed or denied it — see `## Gotchas` for the full narrative
  and `## Verification` for what's now behaviourally confirmed:
  - **`47a597c`** added a hard-ceiling backstop (a grace period forcing
    `RunCompletionGate` to fire if neither side resolved) — but scheduled it
    **at launch**, bounding the entire call and not just the abnormal case,
    which would have killed a real 40-minute HandBrake encode at 31 minutes.
  - **`d866596`** fixed that (grace arms only once one side has reported)
    and pursued the actual leak: `RunCompletionGate` self-retains until it
    fires, and its `onReady` closure captures `process` (and thus `pipe`)
    so neither can be deallocated by the watchdog closures being canceled
    early.
  - **`17a2e24`** fixed two more problems found on review of `d866596`:
    grace could still arm from `markReaderDone()` alone (now only
    `markProcessDone(_:)` arms it), and `onReady` capturing `process` while
    `process.terminationHandler` captures `gate` is a direct retain cycle
    (now broken by `onReady` nilling `terminationHandler` and the watchdog
    variables once it runs).

## Gotchas

- **The hardware captures found two guesses wrong and one disproved**, which
  is the entire reason §2.3's asymmetry rule exists — see `## Verification`
  for the details. Nothing here changed a fallback decision incorrectly:
  the withheld/dormant rows all fail safe into `.toolExited`, which is
  already disc-shaped.
- **A real bug in the test stub, found while capturing the inactivity
  watchdog's timing:** `stub-HandBrakeCLI.sh`'s `SLEEP_SECONDS` originally
  ran a plain `sleep "$SLEEP_SECONDS"`, forking a child that inherited the
  stub's end of the output pipe. Killing only the parent (what the
  inactivity watchdog does) left that child holding the pipe open — no EOF
  — until its own sleep finished, so the drain would only complete ~9
  seconds late rather than hanging forever, but still failing the test's
  `elapsed < 5` assertion. Fixed with `exec sleep`, which replaces the
  script's process image instead of forking, so there is only ever one
  process to signal. Test-only; no production code involved.
- **The leaked-continuation warning — quoted verbatim, an open question for
  the reviewer, not explained.** From gordon's
  `Test-Changeover-2026.09.12_20-55-12--0700.xcresult`
  (`StandardOutputAndStandardError.txt` and the session log), at 20:55:26,
  immediately after `Test encodeSurvivesTheReaderTerminationRaceAndKeepsTheFinalLine() started.`:

  ```
  SWIFT TASK CONTINUATION MISUSE: run(executablePath:arguments:watchdog:readerDelay:onLine:) leaked its continuation without resuming it. This may cause tasks waiting on it to remain suspended forever.
  ```

  This is Swift's runtime check on `withCheckedContinuation` reporting that
  a `CheckedContinuation` was released without ever being resumed — the
  "leaked" form, not the "resumed more than once" form. The named function
  is `ProcessRunner.run` (commit `6143729`). A task awaiting an unresumed
  continuation stays suspended forever, which matches the observed
  58-minute, 0%-CPU stall exactly. **I have not identified which code path
  produced it.** Comparing `ProcessRunner.run` line by line against
  `MakeMKVRipper.runMakeMKV`'s reviewed original
  (`git show 6143729^:Changeover/MakeMKVRipper.swift`) found the
  reader/gate/termination sequencing — the order handlers are armed in, when
  `markReaderDone`/`markProcessDone` are called, the gate's fire-once lock —
  structurally unchanged from the reviewed code. The parts that *are* new in
  the extraction, and that I could not rule out as the cause, are:
  1. The `.inactivity` watchdog branch (a repeating `DispatchSourceTimer` on
     `DispatchQueue.global(qos: .utility)`) — absent from the original,
     which only ever used `.absolute`. The leaking call went through
     `EncodeController`, which is the only caller that uses `.inactivity`.
  2. Going through `EncodeController.encode` at all, rather than calling
     `ProcessRunner.run`/`MakeMKVRipper.runMakeMKV` directly — its `onLine`
     closure does extra synchronous work per line (`HandBrakeFailureClassifier.signature(for:outputPath:)`,
     scheduling a `Task { @MainActor in log(line) }`) that the original
     race tests never exercised.
  3. The specific combination the test uses that no single MakeMKV test
     combines: a >64KB fixture (forces multiple pipe reads) **and** a 30ms
     `readerDelay` on every read **and** 20 repeated iterations in one test
     — versus MakeMKV's tests, which combine at most two of these three at
     once.
  4. A **genuine, confirmed** data race, independent of whether it explains
     this hang: `WatchdogState.timedOut` was read as a plain property from
     `terminationHandler` with no lock around the read, only around
     `markTimedOut()`'s write — no guarantee the reading thread would ever
     observe the write. Fixed in `47a597c`, but a stale/never-observed
     boolean read doesn't itself explain a *resume* never happening.
  None of these four is confirmed as *the* leaking path — they are what
  remains after ruling out a structural difference from the reviewed
  original. **This stays open for the reviewer** rather than closed by this
  draft.
- **`47a597c`'s hard-ceiling backstop was a critical regression: it would
  have killed every real HandBrake encode at 31 minutes.** It scheduled its
  ceiling **at launch**, unconditionally, for `watchdog + hardCeilingGrace`
  (1,800s + 60s for `EncodeController`'s default inactivity bound) — with no
  reset on output, so a genuinely healthy, still-producing encode got killed
  and reported `timedOut: true` the moment that fixed deadline passed. #0018
  measured a real x265 encode at 40m06s; that result is disc-shaped, so it
  would have triggered the MakeMKV fallback, whose second encode would have
  been killed by the same 31-minute wall. Unit tests use fast stubs, so
  nothing caught this before the coordinator read the diff. Never landed
  past that review; fixed in the same session by `d866596`, before any
  gordon run could have exercised it.
- **`d866596`: the corrected mitigation, and the actual leak pursued.**
  - *The launch-time ceiling, fixed.* `RunCompletionGate`'s grace timer no
    longer arms unconditionally at construction — it only starts once one
    side has already reported. A process that is still genuinely running is
    bounded only by `watchdog` itself (which terminates it, guaranteeing
    `terminationHandler` fires); this grace period never touches a call
    before that. `aLongHealthyRunIsNeverBoundedByTheGracePeriod` is meant to
    pin it: a stub producing output every 0.5s for ~6s, well past a 2s
    inactivity bound plus 1s grace (3s combined), should complete normally.
    **Confirmed by falsification F5 (2026-09-12).** Reverting the arming to
    unconditional at construction made
    `aLongHealthyRunIsNeverBoundedByTheGracePeriod` fail — but at **~1.0s**,
    not the originally-predicted "~3s": the falsification could only arm
    `grace` (1s) alone at `init`, since `RunCompletionGate.init` has no
    access to `watchdog`'s bound and reproducing the exact `watchdog +
    grace` formula would need a constructor signature change. The
    regression *class* is confirmed exactly (a healthy run gets killed
    early: `elapsed → 1.002s) >= 5.5` failed, `termination.status → -1`,
    `timedOut → true`); the specific "~3s" figure in the original write-up
    was never exercised and should be read as approximate, not measured.
    With the real fix restored, the same test passed in 6.210s/6.207s
    across two full-suite runs — the full ~6-second healthy workload, not
    cut short.
  - *The actual leak, traced (not confirmed).* Prompted by the coordinator's
    question — "who holds the gate strongly; can both handler closures be
    released before 'both done' is recorded?" — tracing ownership through
    `ProcessRunner.run` found: nothing but the two watchdog closures
    (`absoluteWorkItem`'s item, `inactivityTimer`'s event handler) held a
    strong reference to `process`. Both are canceled from inside
    `terminationHandler`, which fires the moment the process side alone
    finishes — possibly before the reader has drained everything. If that
    cancellation released the *last* strong reference to `process`, ARC
    could deallocate it, releasing `pipe` (held only via
    `process.standardOutput`/`standardError`) before the reader's dispatch
    source delivered its final chunk or EOF — tearing down the one thing
    that would ever call `markReaderDone()`. Neither signal then completes,
    and the continuation leaks. This lines up with why `MakeMKVRipper`'s
    `.absolute`-only tests never hit it: the reader typically drains the
    final chunk essentially simultaneously with process exit, so
    `readerDone` is usually already `true` by the time `terminationHandler`
    fires and cancels anything — the failure mode needs the reader to still
    be mid-flight (asleep in `readerDelay`'s `usleep`) at that exact moment,
    which the leaking test's 30ms delay makes far more likely than any
    MakeMKV test ever combines with a >64KB fixture and 20 repeated runs.
  - *The fix for that trace.* `RunCompletionGate` now self-retains from
    `init` until it fires (`selfRetain = self`, cleared in `fire(with:)`),
    so the gate itself can never be deallocated mid-wait regardless of what
    happens to either handler closure. Its `onReady` closure additionally
    captures `process` directly (`_ = process`), tying `process`'s — and
    thus `pipe`'s — lifetime to the gate's own self-retain instead of to
    the watchdog closures that get canceled early. A new `abandon()` method
    releases the self-retain without calling `onReady`, for the one path
    where `Process.run()` itself throws and the gate never gets used at all
    (needed once `selfRetain` existed, or that path would leak the gate
    itself).
  - **Still not proven to be the exact mechanism, though the full suite now
    passes clean.** This is what static review identified as the most
    probable mechanism, and the fix follows directly from it, but nothing
    has reproduced the *exact* gordon interleaving deterministically to
    prove a leak would still occur without the fix (that would require
    reverting the ownership fix specifically and re-running the original
    20-iteration/30ms-delay scenario under gordon's original concurrent
    load — not attempted, since the fix is already in place and re-breaking
    it just to watch it hang again wasn't judged worth another wait).
    What *is* now observed: two full-suite runs (163/163, both) produced
    **zero** `CONTINUATION MISUSE` warnings, including one run of
    `encodeSurvivesTheReaderTerminationRaceAndKeepsTheFinalLine()` itself
    (7.643s/7.736s — the exact test that hung for 58 minutes before this
    fix). `runReturnsWithinTheHardCeilingWhenAnOrphanKeepsThePipeOpen`
    exercises a related-but-different scenario (the process exits cleanly
    but an orphaned grandchild holds the pipe open) and passed in
    1.020s/1.020s, confirming the grace backstop resolves it, preferring
    the real exit status with `timedOut: true` layered on top.
- **`17a2e24`: two more problems in `d866596`'s design, caught on review
  before any run.**
  1. *Grace could still arm from `markReaderDone()` alone.* If EOF arrived
     before the process actually exited (a child that closes its output but
     keeps running), `d866596`'s version would start the same countdown from
     either side. Expiry in that state reported `timedOut: true` and a
     synthetic status **while the process was still running**, closed the
     pipe on a live child, and would have let the pipeline act (fall back,
     or fail) on a job that hadn't actually finished. `markReaderDone()` now
     never arms grace — with no termination recorded it simply waits — and
     `markProcessDone(_:)` is the only place grace is ever armed.
     `readerDoneAloneNeverArmsGraceOrFires` pins this: the reader reporting
     alone, waited past what a wrongly-armed grace window would have been,
     shows no delivery until `markProcessDone(_:)` is also called.
     **Confirmed by falsification F6 (2026-09-12):** reverting
     `markReaderDone()` to arm grace again made this test fail exactly as
     predicted — the gate fired prematurely with a synthetic
     `Termination(status: -1, uncaughtSignal: false, timedOut: true)` from
     the reader alone. With the fix restored, the test passed in
     0.510s/0.509s across both full-suite runs.
  2. *A `process ↔ gate` retain cycle, on every call, not just a hung one.*
     `onReady` captures `process`; `process.terminationHandler` captures
     `gate` (via `markProcessDone`) and the two watchdog variables, whose own
     closures capture `process` again — a direct cycle, and Darwin does not
     document that `Process` releases `terminationHandler` after firing it.
     Left alone this would leak a `Process`, a `Pipe`, and (for
     `.inactivity`) a timer source on every single successful call, not only
     the hung one being investigated. `onReady` now nils
     `process.terminationHandler` and both watchdog variables the moment it
     runs — safe because fix (1) guarantees `onReady` never runs until
     `markProcessDone(_:)` already has. The same cycle is broken inline in
     the `Process.run()` launch-failure path, which assigns
     `terminationHandler` before ever calling `run()`. **Still not directly
     instrumented** — no leak-detection test (e.g. an `XCTMemoryLeak`-style
     check or a deinit-tracking probe) confirmed the cycle existed or that
     this closes it; the fix follows from static ownership tracing alone.
     Indirect evidence only: two full 163-test runs completed in the same
     process without incident, which a genuine per-call `Process`/`Pipe`
     leak wouldn't necessarily disrupt at this scale (163 tests, one
     process each, isn't enough to exhaust file descriptors even if every
     one leaked). This claim is weaker than the others in this section and
     should be treated as such by the reviewer.
- **The stray test-host processes (`#0019`) are a separate, real, but
  *unproven* finding from the same stuck run — not used here to explain the
  warning above.** Two `Changeover` test-host instances were already running
  on gordon from a previous DerivedData build; one was launched by `launchd`
  23 seconds after a login. `#0019`'s own Notes say plainly this is
  consistent with *either* `AppDelegate.registerLoginItem()` registering a
  test build as a login item *or* macOS's ordinary "reopen windows at login"
  behavior, and the check that would tell them apart (`sudo sfltool
  dumpbtm`) has not been run. `AppDelegate` is out of scope for #0009 and
  has not been touched here.
- **Test runs on gordon were on hold** (coordinator, 2026-09-12) while the
  user decided whether to move test automation to a VM. The hold was lifted
  the same day for unit tests on gordon; the full-suite confirmations and
  F1–F6 recorded in `## Verification` were run after that. (This bullet
  originally said they "remain outstanding"; that was true when written and
  is no longer.)

Both controllers stream the child process's output into the log line by line (`RipController.swift:31-41`, `EncodeController.swift:35-45`) and then **discard it**. Nothing is retained, so when the termination handler fires with a non-zero status there is no material left to explain it with. The user gets a number.

The MakeMKV beta key is the case that matters most in practice: it expires every couple of months, and when it does every rip fails until a new key is entered in MakeMKV.app. The difference between "your key expired, go get a new one" and "status 1" is the difference between a thirty-second fix and an evening of debugging.

There is a second, nastier case. **`makemkvcon` can exit 0 having produced nothing** — some builds print an activation error and still terminate cleanly. Combined with the stale-file selection in [#0003](0003.md), that path is how the wrong movie gets into the library without any error at all.

## Steps to reproduce

1. Let the MakeMKV beta key expire (or set the system clock past its expiry).
2. Insert a disc, search, select, Start Ripping.
3. `makemkvcon` prints its activation error into the streamed log, which scrolls past during the run.
4. The job ends with `"✗ makemkvcon exited with status 1"` and `"✗ Ripping failed. Aborting."`
5. Nothing in the app says the key expired or what to do about it.

## Expected behavior

The final message names the problem: *"MakeMKV's key has expired — open MakeMKV.app and enter a current key, then try again."* Unreadable disc, no disc, and a missing tool are likewise reported as themselves, and the raw tail of tool output stays available in the log for anything unrecognized.

## Actual behavior

Every non-zero exit produces the same message with a different integer, and the tool's own output is unavailable by the time the failure is reported.

  prefixes**; never discard a line just because it *starts* with progress text.

  right for robot mode but wrong for HandBrake.

### 1. The drain fix: extract a shared `ProcessRunner` (decision)

**Decision: extract one process runner from `MakeMKVRipper.runMakeMKV` and use
it in `EncodeController` and `MakeMKVRipper`. Leave `LSDVDIdentity` alone.**

Why a shared runner rather than a local fix in `EncodeController`:

1. **A local fix *is* a copy.** `RunCompletionGate` and `LineSplitter` are
   `private` to `MakeMKVRipper.swift` (`:556`, `:590`). Fixing
   `EncodeController` in place means writing a second implementation of the
   exact synchronization that took #0015 a bounce to get right.
2. **Both readers need the same new fixes:** byte-level carry-over, `\r`, and
   lossy UTF-8 decoding (§0 defects 2–3). Fixing them twice doubles the review
   surface.
3. **The risk to reviewed code is bounded, and the existing tests measure it.**
   `runMakeMKV` keeps its exact signature and return value and becomes a thin
   wrapper. Three race regressions already drive it directly:
   `runMakeMKVSurvivesAReaderTerminationRaceAndKeepsTheFinalMSGLine`,
   `runMakeMKVAlwaysReturnsTheFullTranscriptAcrossRepeatedRuns` and
   `runMakeMKVDrainsATranscriptLargerThanThePipeBufferEvenWithADelayedReader`,
   plus the rest of `MakeMKVFallbackTests` (45). If they all pass unchanged,
   the extraction preserved behaviour, and they then protect `EncodeController`
   too.
4. The one real difference, the watchdog, is a small parameter (below).

**Why not `LSDVDIdentity`.** Its API is synchronous: a 3-second
`DispatchGroup` time box (`LSDVDIdentity.swift:114-124`) called on the
DiskArbitration queue. Bridging an async runner back to sync there means a
semaphore on that queue, which is a new risk in a file that isn't this ticket's.

to `OpticalDiscClassifier.fallbackDiscID`. That never touches a job outcome or a
fallback decision. It already has a carry-over on #0005 (`0005.md`, "Carry-over
from the #0013 review"), where the fix belongs. **Do not edit `LSDVDIdentity`
or `0005.md`.** #0005 may adopt `ProcessRunner` behind a sync wrapper when it
next touches that file.

#### New file `Changeover/ProcessRunner.swift`

```swift
nonisolated enum ProcessRunner {
    nonisolated enum Watchdog: Sendable, Equatable {
        case absolute(TimeInterval)    // kill if still running after this long (MakeMKV: unchanged 4 h)
        case inactivity(TimeInterval)  // kill if no bytes arrive for this long (HandBrake)
    }

    nonisolated struct Termination: Sendable, Equatable {
        let status: Int32              // Process.terminationStatus
        let uncaughtSignal: Bool       // terminationReason == .uncaughtSignal
        let timedOut: Bool             // the watchdog called terminate()
    }

    /// One reader; resumes exactly once, only after BOTH reader EOF and process
    /// exit (RunCompletionGate); flushes the splitter's carry-over before
    /// resuming; never reads after exit. `onLine` gets trimmed, non-empty lines
    /// in order, synchronously on the reader's serial queue.
    /// Failure = `Process.run()` threw; the caller wraps it with its own stage.
    nonisolated static func run(
        executablePath: String,
        arguments:      [String],
        watchdog:       Watchdog,
        readerDelay:    @escaping () -> Void = {},   // test seam, inert in production
        onLine:         @escaping (String) -> Void
    ) async -> Result<Termination, Error>
}
```

- **Move `RunCompletionGate` there verbatim**, as `nonisolated final class …:
  @unchecked Sendable`, internal rather than private.
- **Rewrite `LineSplitter` at the byte level.** Feed it `Data`, keep a `Data`
  carry, split on `0x0A` and `0x0D`, and decode each complete line with
  `String(decoding:as: UTF8.self)`, which is lossy and never returns `nil`.
  Empty lines are dropped after trimming `.whitespaces`, so `\r\n`, and a `\r`
  ending one chunk followed by `\n` starting the next, just produce an empty line
  that's skipped. `flush()` decodes the remaining carry. Make it internal so it
  can be unit-tested directly.
- **`LineAccumulator` stays in `MakeMKVRipper.swift`.** Only the ripper needs the
  whole transcript. `EncodeController` must not accumulate a 40-minute encode.
- **Watchdog.** `.absolute` is today's `DispatchWorkItem` (`:523-528`),
  unchanged. `.inactivity(t)`: record `lastActivity` under a lock on every
  non-empty chunk. A `DispatchSourceTimer` on a utility queue checks every
  `min(t/4, 30 s)` and calls `terminate()` once idle ≥ `t`. Set `timedOut`
  under the same lock, and cancel the timer in `terminationHandler`.
- **`runMakeMKV` keeps its signature** (`MakeMKVRipper.swift:472-478`). Body:
  a `LineAccumulator`, then
  `ProcessRunner.run(…, watchdog: .absolute(hangTimeout), readerDelay: readerDelay) { accumulator.append($0); onLine($0) }`.
  Map `.success(t)` → `.success((t.status, accumulator.snapshot()))` and
  `.failure(e)` → `JobFailure(stage: .rip, reason: .launchFailure(toolPath:error:))`.
  Keep the long doc comment's race explanation, pointed at `ProcessRunner`.
- **Behaviour change for MakeMKV, stated so the reviewer can check it:** `\r` is
  now a separator (every `makemkvcon` fixture has zero `\r`, so line counts are
  unchanged: 3934 for the 112 KB fixture), and a chunk split inside a UTF-8
  sequence no longer drops the chunk. That's all.

### 2. The classifier

New file `Changeover/HandBrakeFailureClassifier.swift`. It's pure, has no
`Process` and no file I/O, and is `nonisolated` throughout.

```swift
nonisolated enum HandBrakeFailureClassifier {
    nonisolated enum SignatureID: String, Sendable, CaseIterable {
        case noSpaceLeft, outputOpenFailed, unrecognizedOption, invalidEncoder,
             encodeCanceled, cssKeyFailure, cssUnavailable, readError,
             dvdStructureUnreadable, noTitleFound
    }
    nonisolated struct Match: Sendable, Equatable {
        let id: SignatureID
        let reason: FailureReason
        let line: String            // verbatim, for presentation (§4)
    }
    nonisolated struct Input: Sendable {
        let termination: ProcessRunner.Termination
        let lines: [String]         // evidence lines + tail (§3)
        let outputPath: String      // the --output file
        let outputIsNonEmpty: Bool  // measured by EncodeController after exit
        let availableCapacity: Int64?   // output volume, measured after exit; nil = unknown
    }

    /// Per-line: the signature this line matches, or nil. Substring, case-sensitive.
    nonisolated static func signature(for line: String, outputPath: String) -> Match?
    /// nil means success. Never inspects `lines` for a clean exit that produced a file.
    nonisolated static func classify(_ input: Input) -> FailureReason?
    /// The highest-precedence match in `lines` whose reason equals `reason`, for §4.
    nonisolated static func evidence(in lines: [String], for reason: FailureReason, outputPath: String) -> Match?
}
```

`EncodeController` measures `outputIsNonEmpty` and `availableCapacity` (the same
`volumeAvailableCapacityForImportantUsageKey` read as
`MakeMKVRipper.availableCapacity`, on the output's parent) *after* the process
exits, and passes plain values in. Both are skipped for a clean exit that
produced a file.

#### 2.2 Precedence

`classify` evaluates tiers in order and returns the first tier with a match;
within a tier, the first matching row. Why this order:

- **Tier 0 first.** A watchdog `terminate()` makes HandBrake print its own
  cancel text. That must not become `.cancelled` and switch the fallback off.
- **Tiers 1–3 (non-disc) before tier 4 (disc).** A real-disc run always carries
  libdvdread noise, and may carry incidental read retries. If the disk filled,
  the output couldn't be opened, or HandBrake rejected its arguments, those are
  the cause. A MakeMKV rip onto the same volume, or a second encode with the same
  arguments, fails the same way 20+ minutes later.
- **Tier 4 before tier 5.** "No title found" is usually the *consequence* of an
  unreadable IFO or a failed CSS key. Both tiers are disc-shaped, so the order
  changes only the message, and the cause reads better.
- **The capacity probe sits in tier 1** but only runs for exit ≠ 0. It catches
  disk-full even if HandBrake's wording differs from C4.

#### 2.3 The asymmetry rule: what may ship unconfirmed

- **A row mapping to a *disc-shaped* reason may ship unconfirmed.** Until it
  fires, the job is `.toolExited`, which is also disc-shaped, so an unconfirmed
  row can change the message but never the fallback decision. Its worst case is
  a slightly wrong sentence, with the raw tail still in the log.
- **A row mapping to a *non-disc* reason ships only when a real capture (§5)
  contains the line.** A wrong match there silently denies a real bad disc its
  MakeMKV fallback. If a capture can't be made, leave the row out of the code,
  keep it in this table, and say so in `## Verification`.
- **`outputOpenFailed` is anchored to the output path. Never match a bare
  `Permission denied`.** libdvdcss's raw-device open failure is itself a
  permission error on `/dev/rdiskN`. A bare match would classify every real disc
  failure as `.destinationUnwritable` and switch the fallback off everywhere.
  §6 test 3 pins this.

#### 2.4 Exit 0

- **A clean exit that produced a non-empty output file → `nil` (success).
  `lines` are not inspected at all.** This is how an `ERROR:` line in a
  successful run can never become a failure. It is structural, not a pattern
  exclusion, and §6 test 5 pins it.
- **Exit 0 but the output file is missing or empty → a failure.** If
  `noTitleFound` matches, the reason is `.noTitlesProduced`. Otherwise it is
  `.unknown("HandBrakeCLI exited successfully but wrote no output file")`. Both
  are disc-shaped. This is the HandBrake successor to the filing's
  "`makemkvcon` exits 0 having produced nothing". C6 records whether HandBrake
  1.11.2 even exits 0 for "No title found". Either way the classification holds.
- **Exit 0 with a *truncated or corrupt* file is out of scope.** It needs a
  duration check against the title (Phase 2, [#0025](0025.md)). See §10.

#### 2.5 How `.toolExited` narrows, and the one new case

- **Unmatched non-zero exits stay `.toolExited(code:)`.** "Unclassified" means
  exactly that. The code is kept (useful once exit codes are understood) and the
  raw tail is already in `logTail`. `.unknown(String)` is **not** the unmatched
  bucket. It is reserved for anomalies Changeover observes itself, each with a
  sentence: the watchdog, exit 0 with no output, and the ripper's existing uses.
  Update the comment on `.unknown` in `FallbackPolicy.isDiscShaped`
  (`FallbackPolicy.swift:43-45`, "#0009's unmatched non-zero exit is today's
  `.toolExited`") to say this. The comment changes; the behaviour doesn't.
- A signal death (`uncaughtSignal`, e.g. a libdvdnav segfault on a malformed
  IFO) with no match stays `.toolExited(code: <signal>)`: disc-shaped, the same
  as today. The presenter says "stopped unexpectedly" rather than
  "exited with status" when the tail's final line is not HandBrake's normal
  `HandBrake has exited.`. No new case for this.

**New case: `FailureReason.toolIncompatible(detail: String)`.** Adding it is a
wire change ([#0060](0060.md)), so here is the case for it:

1. **No existing case is honest.** `.toolLaunchFailed` means `Process.run()`
   threw (`JobOutcome.swift:37`). `.unknown` and `.toolExited` are both
   disc-shaped, so a HandBrake that rejects Changeover's arguments would run a
   7 GB MakeMKV rip and a second encode that fails identically, **on every
   disc**, until someone notices.
2. **The realistic trigger is ordinary.** A `brew upgrade handbrake` that
   renames or drops a flag Changeover passes (`--encoder-preset`, `--aencoder
   copy:aac,copy:ac3`), or a build without x265. For [#0040](0040.md)'s
   unattended queue that is a whole night of wasted fallbacks.
3. **It is cheap now and expensive later.** #0060 has not shipped, and no peer
   decodes `FailureReason` yet (#0007's note on unknown-case decoding still
   applies). The associated value is **labelled** (`detail:`) to avoid the `_0`
   key #0007 flagged.
4. **#0008 can reuse it** for a HandBrakeCLI version probe at `.preflight`.

Wiring: add the case after `.toolLaunchFailed` with a doc comment. Add it to
`FallbackPolicy.isDiscShaped` beside `.toolMissing, .toolLaunchFailed` → `false`
(the exhaustive switch won't compile until you do). Add it to
`JobOutcomeTests.outcomeRoundTripsThroughCodable`'s all-cases list.
`DiscReliabilityLog` uses `String(describing:)`, so it needs nothing. **No other
case is added.** The CSS/read-error distinction lives in `Match.id` for
presentation, not in the wire vocabulary (§4).

### 3. What `JobFailure.logTail` holds for an encode failure

`LogTailBuffer` keeps 40 lines (`JobOutcome.swift:135`). HandBrake's post-failure
epilogue can push the decisive line out of 40. In the success fixture, from
`sync: reached video pts` (`:495`) to `HandBrake has exited.` (`:537`) is 43
lines: x264 stats, mux, `work result`, `Encode done!`. So:

- **Tail:** a `LogTailBuffer(capacity: 40)` that skips *progress-only* lines. A
  line is progress-only if it starts with `Encoding: task`, `Scanning title` or
  `Muxing:` **and** contains no `] `, the timestamped fragment that interleaving
  glues on (`:147`, `:163`, `:507` keep their log text). Progress still goes to
  the UI log.
- **Evidence:** a second small bounded buffer (last 5) of lines for which
  `signature(for:outputPath:)` is non-nil, appended as they stream.
- **`logTail` = evidence lines not already in the tail, oldest first, then the
  tail.** At most 45 lines. The wire type is unchanged. Update the
  `JobFailure.logTail` doc comment. `classify` receives the same array, so for
  every text row **re-classifying `failure.logTail` reproduces
  `failure.reason`**. A remote client ([#0060](0060.md)) can re-derive the
  evidence line. §6 test 11 pins it.
- The launch-failure and `.destinationUnwritable`-before-launch paths keep
  passing `tail.snapshot()` as today (empty in practice).

### 9. Risks

- **Misclassifying *out of* the fallback** is the expensive error: a real bad disc
  is denied MakeMKV. Mitigations: non-disc rows ship only with a capture (§2.3);
  `outputOpenFailed` is anchored to the output path (test 7); tiers 1–3 must
  match text that can't come from disc reading; F2 and F4.
- **Misclassifying *into* the fallback** costs one wasted attempt, exactly as
  today's `.toolExited` does, so it is no regression. `.toolIncompatible` and
  `.diskFull` remove the two cases that would repeat on every disc.
- **The capacity probe's false positive:** a genuine disc failure on a volume
  with under 256 MB free is reported as `.diskFull` and not retried. Accepted:
  `MakeMKVRipper.rip` would refuse anyway below `size + 1 GiB`
  (`MakeMKVRipper.swift:119-124`).
- **HandBrake wording drifts across versions.** An unmatched line degrades to
  `.toolExited`, today's behaviour. Filenames pin captures to `hb1.11.2`.
  Re-capture on upgrade.
- **The inactivity watchdog kills a legitimately silent phase** (a drive
  retrying one sector for 30 minutes). The result is disc-shaped, so the fallback
  runs. That is arguably correct, and preferable to hanging forever with no
  cancel (#0001 G7).
- **Touching reviewed code (`MakeMKVRipper.runMakeMKV`).** Bounded by an
  unchanged signature, 45 unmodified tests, and a separate commit A.
- **Progress filtering hides a real line.** Only lines with no `] ` fragment are
  skipped, they are still logged, and evidence lines are kept separately.
- **An inherited pipe end.** If a tool ever spawned a child that kept the pipe's
  write end open, EOF would never arrive after exit and the gate would wait
  forever. Neither tool does. Recorded, not fixed.
- **English-only strings.** Fine for Phase 1. Localizing is [#0100](0100.md)-era
  work.

## Re-triage

**2026-09-11 — scope change: the expiring key stops being the headline failure.**
Per `MakeMKVReplacement-Path.md` §4 ("Changed in scope"). The ticket stays `open`
and the classifier is still wanted; the signature list changes and the priorities
invert.

After [#0014](0014.md) and [#0015](0015.md), `makemkvcon` is not invoked at all on
a disc HandBrake can handle, so an expired MakeMKV key degrades one fallback rather
than failing every rip. `FailureReason.activationExpired` keeps its meaning and
keeps its place in the vocabulary — it just stops being the case that matters most
in practice.

**The new headline is the libdvdcss raw-device fallback**
(`MakeMKVReplacement-Results.md` §5). libdvdcss could not open the raw device and
silently fell back to retrieving CSS keys through the mounted filesystem:

```
libdvdread: Could not open /dev/disk6 with libdvdcss.
libdvdread: Can't open /dev/disk6 for reading
libdvdread: Attempting to retrieve all CSS keys
libdvdread: This can take a _long_ time, please be patient
libdvdread: Get key for /VIDEO_TS/VIDEO_TS.VOB at 0x0000013c
   … 17 keys, every one at Elapsed time 0 …
libdvdread: Found 4 VTS's
```

Raw `/dev/disk6` access needs privileges the process does not have. It worked here,
instantly — but the job is running on a path libdvdread itself describes as slow
and last-resort, and *"a disc where the fallback does not hold would fail, and the
failure would be a CSS error rather than an obvious one."* It is also the most
plausible explanation for any future disc that works in MakeMKV but not HandBrake.
That makes it the single signature most worth recognizing and naming, and the one
whose explanation most needs to be actionable rather than a raw CSS error.

Other HandBrake-side signatures to add, all observed or documented:

- **`ERROR: unable to decode subtitle with 2019 bytes.`** — non-fatal. Appeared on
  the very first disc tested and the encode completed. A classifier that treats any
  `ERROR` line as a failure is wrong on disc one.
- **An incomplete scan reported as success** — the `--title 0` omission
  ([#0023](0023.md)). Exit 0, valid JSON, one title. Not an error string at all,
  which is why it needs a sanity check rather than a signature.
- **Exit 0 with a corrupt or truncated output file.**
  `MakeMKVReplacement-Results.md` §11: MakeMKV has `io_ErrorRetryCount` /
  `io_IgnoreReadErrors`; HandBrake's equivalent is uncharacterized, and *"a corrupt
  rip still produces a file, so spot-check playback, not exit codes."* This is the
  successor to the existing `makemkvcon`-exits-0-having-produced-nothing case and
  is nastier, because there *is* an output file.

**New responsibility from [#0015](0015.md):** the classifier decides which failures
are disc-shaped enough to justify the MakeMKV fallback. A missing HandBrakeCLI
binary, an unwritable output path and a cancelled job are not disc problems, and
retrying them through MakeMKV wastes 40 minutes to fail the same way. That
determination belongs here, not duplicated as string matching in the pipeline.

Unchanged: classification stays strictly enrichment on top of the exit code;
`.unknown` with the raw tail stays a valid, informative outcome; the buffer stays
bounded; and the signature list is an educated guess until confirmed against real
output on this machine, which `## Verification` must state either way.

## Prerequisite found during #0015 review — trailing HandBrake output can be dropped — 2026-09-12

The #0015 phase 3 review found and reproduced a reader/termination race in
`MakeMKVRipper.runMakeMKV`: process output was lost whenever the exit handler ran
before the reader finished. `EncodeController` has the same shape:

- `EncodeController.swift:99-100` — the `readabilityHandler` reads `availableData`
  and appends lines to the `LogTailBuffer` (`:88`) and the log.
- `EncodeController.swift:123-124` — the `terminationHandler` sets
  `readabilityHandler = nil` immediately, with **no final drain** of the pipe,
  then resumes the continuation from the exit status (`:128`, `:135`).

Output HandBrake writes just before exiting can therefore miss `logTail` and the
log. **Today no job outcome changes**, because success or failure comes from the
exit status and the only parsed line (`Found main feature title N`, `:113`)
appears early.

**It matters for this issue**: #0009 classifies failures from `JobFailure.logTail`,
and the lines at risk are HandBrake's final error output — the lines the
classifier needs most. Classifying from an incomplete tail would misclassify
failures and, through `FallbackPolicy` (#0015), take the wrong fallback decision.

**Fix before or as part of this issue:** don't disable the reader without draining.
Resume only after both pipe EOF and process termination, keep a timeout that kills
a hung child, and never read only after exit (large output would fill the ~64 KB
pipe buffer and deadlock, the bug #0013 fixed). The #0015 re-pass rewrites
`MakeMKVRipper.runMakeMKV` to exactly this shape. Once it lands, use it as the
reference, or extract a shared process runner so `EncodeController`,
`MakeMKVRipper` and `LSDVDIdentity` stop carrying three copies of the same
pattern. `LSDVDIdentity`'s milder version is already noted on #0005.
